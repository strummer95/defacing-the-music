# Notes: episode-01 (The Who on The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour, 1967)

Research method: WebSearch only (WebFetch could not reach outside hosts in this session), so everything below comes from search-result summaries and snippets, not full page reads.

## Key facts used, with sources
- The Who played The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour at CBS Television City, Los Angeles. Taped Friday Sept 15, 1967; aired Sunday Sept 17, 1967 (most outlets date it by the air date): https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/flashback-the-who-blow-up-smothers-brothers-in-primetime-20160304 , https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/september-17/the-who-spark-an-explosion-on-national-television
- First US national TV appearance for The Who; two songs, "I Can See for Miles" and "My Generation"; Tommy Smothers chats with the band before "My Generation": https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/september-17/the-who-spark-an-explosion-on-national-television , https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/who-us-tv-debut-smothers-brothers-1967-townshend-moon-daltrey-entwistle/
- Moon packed extra explosive into his bass drum; the blast singed Townshend's hair and left shrapnel in Moon's arm: https://www.history.com/this-day-in-history/september-17/the-who-spark-an-explosion-on-national-television , https://www.slashfilm.com/1545384/the-who-60s-tv-explosion-the-smothers-brothers-comedy-hour
- Who loaded the extra powder is disputed: Moon bribing/asking a stagehand, Moon adding more when the crew wasn't looking, or the stage manager adding powder without knowing Moon already had. Producer Allan Blye said the rehearsal charge was "a powder puff", he asked for a bigger effect, and the band had told their own guy the same thing: https://www.slashfilm.com/1545384/the-who-60s-tv-explosion-the-smothers-brothers-comedy-hour , https://bestclassicbands.com/keith-moon-smothers-brothers-the-who-9-17-155/
- Townshend: "My hair caught fire, and my hearing was never the same again"; he links it to his tinnitus, but has also blamed headphones and decades of loud shows: https://www.musicradar.com/artists/my-hair-caught-fire-and-my-hearing-was-never-the-same-again-when-keith-moon-overdid-the-pyrotechnics-for-the-whos-most-explosive-television-moment
- Townshend took Tommy Smothers' acoustic guitar and smashed it (on tape). One outlier (Diffuser) credits Daltrey; ignore: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/flashback-the-who-blow-up-smothers-brothers-in-primetime-20160304
- 1967 North American tour opening for Herman's Hermits (from July, about ten weeks): https://americansongwriter.com/how-the-whos-1967-us-tour-was-marked-with-piranhas-shotguns-and-exploding-toilets-and-yes-keith-moon-was-involved-in-all-three , https://99wfmk.com/herman-and-the-who/
- Herman's Hermits members introduced Moon to cherry bombs on that tour; he used them on hotel toilets (Terry Fletcher biography quoted): https://americansongwriter.com/how-the-whos-1967-us-tour-was-marked-with-piranhas-shotguns-and-exploding-toilets-and-yes-keith-moon-was-involved-in-all-three , https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-who-drummer-keith-moon-bathroom-bomber/
- Flint, Michigan, Holiday Inn, Aug 23, 1967 (Moon born Aug 23, 1946, so it really was his 21st): cakes (one source says around 200 sent by fans), food fight, fire extinguishers, damage bill (commonly given as $24,000): https://www.wkar.org/season-1/2024-08-23/keith-moons-memorable-flint-birthday-august-23 , https://faroutmagazine.co.uk/the-who-keith-moon-banned-holiday-inn/
- "I Can See for Miles" peaked at No. 9 on the Billboard Hot 100, The Who's only US top 10 single and biggest US hit: https://www.udiscovermusic.com/stories/whos-biggest-us-hitand-infamous-tv-appearance
- CBS pulled the Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour in April 1969 after fights over censorship, politics and the Vietnam War (official reason: missed tape delivery deadlines for censor review); the brothers sued and won: https://fox11online.com/news/entertainment/the-smothers-brothers-mark-their-cbs-firing-50-years-ago
- Keith Moon died in 1978 (eleven years later): general knowledge, not separately searched.

## "Story goes" pile
- Car in the Holiday Inn pool (Lincoln, Cadillac or Rolls-Royce depending on who tells it). Moon reportedly told it himself in a 1972 Rolling Stone interview; most later writers call it a myth: https://www.musicradar.com/news/keith-moon-rolls-royce-pool , https://ultimateclassicrock.com/keith-moon-car-into-pool/
- Holiday Inn lifetime ban: widely repeated, not firmly documented.
- Bette Davis fainting into Mickey Rooney's arms backstage: reported in retellings; one article says the Smothers Brothers themselves have denied it. The script already frames it as a legend, which is right: https://www.slashfilm.com/1545384/the-who-60s-tv-explosion-the-smothers-brothers-comedy-hour
- "Ten times" the normal flash powder: one account. The script attributes it to "one account", which is fine.

## Nov 14 cleanup pass: taping-day eyewitness moments added (WebSearch summaries only; hollywoodreporter.com, loudersound.com and bestclassicbands.com were not reachable for full reads)
- Townshend, VH1 interview (as quoted by Rolling Stone): the Smothers Brothers had a "fairly radical" show and "bravely asked us if we would destroy our instruments"; also "It set my hair alight." Used in THE SMOTHERS BROTHERS and THE BOOM: https://www.rollingstone.com/music/news/flashback-the-who-blow-up-smothers-brothers-in-primetime-20160304
- Tommy Smothers, Hollywood Reporter oral history (Marc Freeman, Nov 25 2017, "'The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour' at 50: The Rise and Fall of a Groundbreaking Variety Show"): the bit was supposed to end with Townshend smashing his guitar; "there was shrapnel everywhere"; he thought there were going to be dead bodies; because he was looking around panicked, people assumed it wasn't part of the act; "a real, authentic moment." Used in THE GUITAR (the script now says the guitar smash was the planned bit and the bomb was not): https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/live-feed/smothers-brothers-comedy-hour-oral-history-1060153
- Dick Smothers, same oral history: he stood in the wings with Bette Davis watching; "The rumor was that she fainted when she heard the blast"; "Bette Davis could see an atom bomb go off and wouldn't faint. I don't even know if she blinked." Tommy Smothers has also denied the faint (/Film). Bette Davis and Mickey Rooney were on the bill that week. Used in THE LEGEND, which now calls the faint debunked by the two hosts: https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/live-feed/smothers-brothers-comedy-hour-oral-history-1060153 , https://www.slashfilm.com/1545384/the-who-60s-tv-explosion-the-smothers-brothers-comedy-hour , https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-who-the-smothers-brothers
- One account puts the fainting exaggeration down to Moon retelling it years later (search summary; framed as "some people think" in a FILL).
- Roger Daltrey, Ultimate Classic Rock 2024 interview: "It blew me flat on my face." Used in THE BOOM: https://ultimateclassicrock.com/roger-daltrey-interview-2024/
- The blast also took out a camera and the studio's monitors (Rolling Stone, The Kids Are Alright summary; framed "reportedly"): https://www.rollingstone.com/music/music-news/the-kids-are-alright-fifteen-years-of-the-who-238404/
- Entwistle barely flinching and glancing back in the footage (Louder description; viewer can check it on the clip): https://www.loudersound.com/features/the-who-the-smothers-brothers

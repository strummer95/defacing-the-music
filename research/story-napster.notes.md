# Notes: story-napster (Due To Unauthorized Downloading / Who Brought Lars Ulrich the Boxes?)

Written Nov 27, 2026. New script (was an idea note). Research method: WebSearch, plus WebFetch/curl of the pages themselves where they were open. Quotes marked EXACT were copied from the page text; anything taken only from a search-result summary is marked SUMMARY and is framed in the script as "reported" or kept out of quote marks where possible. The SF Chronicle article is paywalled (402) and was not read directly.

Show rules applied: no copyright politics or takes, no AI music anything, the 12-year-old sued in 2003 is NOT named and is never the joke (she was named in 2003 press, but she is a private person).

## Shawn Fanning and the start of Napster
- Taught himself Unix programming between junior and senior years at Harwich High, Cape Cod; "He never went back to his dorm room, leaving behind his clothes, books and bedding."; nickname "the Napster (a reference to his perpetually nappy hair)" from high school. EXACT. Time, "Meet the Napster", Karl Taro Greenfeld, Oct 2, 2000: https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,998068-3,00.html
- Fanning to Spin: "My roommates were MP3 fanatics," ... "I heard them complaining all the time about how hard it was trying to find songs." Napster took 10% of network resources in November and 50% by January; by March 1 more than 130 universities had pulled the plug. Uncle John gave him a computer, office space. EXACT (via WebFetch). https://www.spinmagazine.com/2011/12/farewell-napster-read-spins-2000-story-about-little-pirate-could/ . The 10%/50% figure is for one network (the article's college section); script says "at one school".
- Fortune oral history (Richard Nieva, Sept 5, 2013): nickname from a basketball-court trash talker making fun of his hair; IRC channel w00w00; company in Hull, Mass.; Jordan Ritter "Dude, my uncle just incorporated the company."; San Mateo office in a converted bank building, Richardson "if you pushed someone's chair back, you'd hit someone else's chair"; "Let's change it to 'Rapster!' Which makes no sense whatsoever."; Ron Conway's party, Page and Brin: "Wow, we'll never be as famous as that Fanning guy standing over there."; Hilary Rosen / Eileen Richardson Billboard phone call (read in full in script); RIAA sued Dec 6, 1999; Limp Bizkit free tour summer 2000; "There was actual nerd dancing" (Ritter, on the appeals court stay); $1 billion Bertelsmann offer Feb 2001; Kessler "We hired probably 50 temps"; Gracenote's Stephen White "The band Aha was a big one."; Parker email "...especially since they are exchanging pirated music."; Jeff Knowles (attorney for the song publishers) "How many cases do you have an email that's essentially a confession?"; Napster "Thanks for sharing" shirts; Napster execs/RIAA side quotes. EXACT. https://fortune.com/2013/09/05/ashes-to-ashes-peer-to-peer-an-oral-history-of-napster/
  - Caveat: Fortune itself says these are recollections and some contradict each other. Fortune dates the Senate hearing July 12 and the shutdown July 1, 2001; the Senate transcript says July 11, 2000 (used), and Stereogum says the network shut down July 11, 2001, so the script just says "July 2001".
- Sean Parker met Fanning online as teenagers; raised the first $50,000; launched June 1999; first president of Facebook; played by Justin Timberlake; called the character a "morally reprehensible human being" and "it's hard to complain about being played by a sex symbol"; invested $15M in Spotify in 2010. Wikipedia, Sean Parker: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Parker
- Beta June 1, 1999; Snocap, Rupture (to EA), Path, Airtime (2012 launch event, product repeatedly crashed); cameo as himself in The Italian Job (2003), Seth Green's character says Fanning stole Napster while he napped in their Northeastern dorm; VMA 2000 Metallica shirt, "For Whom the Bell Tolls" playing, said a friend shared the shirt with him, Lars shown feigning boredom. Wikipedia, Shawn Fanning: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Fanning . The shirt line's exact wording varies by source ("a friend of mine shared it with me" / "a friend shared it with me!" / "I borrowed this shirt from a friend..."), so the script paraphrases it without quote marks.
- Peak use: 26.4 million unique users in February 2001 (Jupiter Media Metrix, 14 countries). https://www.clickz.com/napster-users-fan-out-in-search-of-file-swapping-apps/83377/

## "I Disappear" and the lawsuit
- Lars at the 92nd Street Y, Nov 5, 2017: Cliff Burnstein's call, "'I Disappear' is being played on 20 radio stations across America", "It lives in our vault somewhere", "Well, let's go fuck with Napster then."; "We were all tape traders, and we were totally pro all this stuff..."; "That was a dare..." "...started pulling 335,000 names out of a pickup truck..." "maybe not the smartest PR move of all time, but at least we won the argument."; "Sean Parker and I are best friends"; Blabbermouth says he attended Parker's wedding. EXACT. https://blabbermouth.net/news/lars-ulrich-admits-delivering-names-of-napster-users-was-maybe-not-the-smartest-pr-move-of-all-time , https://loudwire.com/metallica-lars-ulrich-we-could-have-educated-ourselves-better-about-napster/
- Who leaked "I Disappear" has never been established (I Disappear Wikipedia and several retrospectives). Song released June 2, 2000. On April 23, 2021 Metallica released "Disappear (Leaked and Live)" through their Vinyl Club; track one "I Disappear (Leaked Napster Version)". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Disappear
- Suit filed April 13, 2000, U.S. District Court, Central District of California (Los Angeles); also named USC, Yale, Indiana University; copyright, digital audio interface device, RICO; Lars press release ("trafficking in stolen goods"); Pulgram: "It is also hard to understand why plaintiffs -- a group located in the San Francisco Bay Area -- saw it necessary to file a separate action in Los Angeles." (script reads the dashes as commas). EXACT. Wired, Christopher Jones: https://www.wired.com/2000/04/metallica-rips-napster/
- At least $10 million, $100,000 per song (CNET, via Wikipedia): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallica_v._Napster,_Inc.
- Hackers hit Metallica's site with "LEAVE NAPSTER ALONE"; Napster execs alerted users, who chanted "Fuck you, Lars!"; network shut down July 2001. Stereogum, Nate Patrin, Apr 13, 2020: https://www.stereogum.com/2079981/metallica-sues-napster-2000/franchises/columns/sounding-board/

## The boxes (May 3, 2000)
- 335,435 user IDs (screen names, not real names), about 60,000 pages, compiled by NetPD (British consulting firm) over the weekend of April 28 to 30; delivered to Napster's San Mateo HQ by Lars and attorney Howard King. CBS/AP: Ulrich "helped wheel in 60,000 pages"; fans broke Metallica CDs and waved banners; Tower Records quote; Fanning statement "I'm a huge Metallica fan and therefore really sorry that they're going in this direction."; Pulgram statement. EXACT. https://www.cbsnews.com/news/between-a-rock-band-a-hard-drive/ , https://www.deseret.com/2000/5/4/19504719/metallica-says-list-identifies-music-pirates/
- "13 boxes" on "handcarts"; fans shouting "Long live Napster" and "We just want to hear the music." SUMMARY of the SF Chronicle "Sour Notes Over Napster" (paywalled, not read directly) plus a report quoted on Plagiarism Today ("13 boxes containing 335,435 user IDs listed on 60,000 sheets of paper"). Script attributes "Long live Napster" to the Chronicle. https://www.sfgate.com/business/article/Sour-Notes-Over-Napster-Downloading-of-free-3240388.php
- Lars's own 2017 memory is a pickup truck; period reports say handcarts. Both can be true (truck to the curb, carts inside); the script uses both, each attributed.
- Napster banned about 317,000 accounts (May 10); about 30,000 counter-notifications (Metallica said 17,000); Howard King to Salon: "They're perjuring themselves... Could there be 10 mistakes? Sure. 15? Maybe. But there are not 17,000 mistakes. There's no way." EXACT. Salon, "Napster fans to Metallica: Prove it!", May 16, 2000: https://www.salon.com/2000/05/16/metallica_backlash/
- Ban beaten "simply by fiddling around with Windows' Registry file and reinstalling Napster's software"; Dre's list got 230,142 more booted, "Napster Exiles" just under 550,000. EXACT. The Register, June 1, 2000: https://www.theregister.com/2000/06/01/napster_exiles_230_000_more/

- Official transcript (Senate Hearing 106-1060), chair Orrin Hatch: "the Gnutella download is complete... We searched for music from a popular rock group called Creed... We downloaded just one of these songs."; "Senator Leahy and I want to hear every one of you, so we are going to go vote while you listen to one of these songs. Is that all right? [Laughter.]"; "I have to say that I was listening to Metallica this morning in my office. [Laughter.]"; "you could use my lyricist ability" / "the 2,000 that we normally sell" (Hatch's own songwriting); recess 10:26 to 10:41 a.m.; Lars's statement incl. "five or six versions of our work in progress" and "Napster hijacked our music without asking."; Hank Barry introduces Fanning, "19 today... sitting right behind me". EXACT. https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CHRG-106shrg74728/html/CHRG-106shrg74728.htm

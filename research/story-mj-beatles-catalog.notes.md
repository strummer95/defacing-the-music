# Research notes: Due To a Hot Business Tip / Who Taught Michael Jackson About Publishing?

Written Nov 27, 2026. Business story only. Nothing about the allegations against Michael Jackson, by design (CLAUDE.md hard rule). Also left out on purpose: Neverland, his debts, the 2005 trial testimony about spending, anything tied to the 1990s settlements. When the Sony section mentions money, it is only the deal itself.

## The friendship
- First meeting: a party on the Queen Mary, Long Beach. Michael's own account (via Wikipedia "Girlfriend (Paul McCartney song)"): Paul told him he'd written a song for him. Year not pinned down by the sources I found (often said 1975, the Venus and Mars launch party); script says "in the seventies".
- "Girlfriend": Wings, London Town (1978). Quincy Jones suggested it for Off the Wall (1979) without knowing Paul had already offered it. Source: Wikipedia, Girlfriend.
- Paul on Letterman, July 15, 2009 (transcript, Celebitchy; also excerpted in Wikipedia Sony Music Publishing, dated there as July 2009):
  - Michael "called him up on Christmas Day one year and asked 'Do you want to make some hits?'" (paraphrase by the writer, Michael's line in quotes)
  - Paul: "So I said yeah, sure, you know being of the hit-making variety."
  - "He came to my house, and we got to know the family and stuff"
  - "It actually kind of fell apart a little bit later because he was talking to me and asking my business advice and one of the things I said to him was 'Think about getting into music publishing,' and then he looked at me and I thought he was joking and he said 'I'm going to get yours.'"
  - "I thought, 'He is the guy historically placed to give Lennon/McCartney a good deal at last.'" (the transcript has a typo "Leno/McCartney"; Wikipedia's version reads "Lennon–McCartney")
  - "We got signed when we were 21 or something in a back alley in Liverpool"
  - "So I kept thinking it was time for a raise... You would, you know? I did talk to him about it. He kept saying 'That's just business Paul.'"
  - "We never got to it, so we kind of drifted apart. It was no big bust up."
- "Say Say Say": No. 1 for six weeks, late 1983 into 1984 (Pop History Dig). Video shot Oct 4 to 7, 1983, Los Alamos (Santa Barbara County), California, directed by Bob Giraldi; Paul and Michael play con men "Mac and Jack" selling a "Wonder Potion" (Paul McCartney Project; Wikipedia).
- Michael stayed at Paul and Linda's during the 1981 sessions (Wikipedia Sony Music Publishing, citing Taraborrelli).

## The binder dinner
- Around 1981, at the McCartney home outside London, Paul showed Michael a thick book/printout of the songs he owned the publishing on (Wikipedia via Taraborrelli; Forbes/Greenburg; Pop History Dig).
- Greenburg (Forbes excerpt of "Michael Jackson, Inc.", 2014): Paul: "This is what I do. I bought the Buddy Holly catalogue, a Broadway catalogue." (This is Greenburg's reconstruction of the dinner. Script attributes it to his book.)
- MPL's holdings in Dec 1981 included the music to Grease, Annie and A Chorus Line (The Times, Dec 1981, via Paul McCartney Project).
- Michael was born Aug 29, 1958, so 22 in mid-1981 (cold open).
- The widely repeated "Great. Good joke." reply appears in Wikipedia (Taraborrelli section) but I couldn't confirm the exact source, so the script uses Paul's own Letterman wording instead ("I thought he was joking").
- Also left out: the "buy the rug they're standing on" line. It's quoted everywhere but I couldn't find which interview or year it came from.

## How the Beatles lost the songs (1969)
- Northern Songs was set up in 1963 by Dick James to hold the Lennon-McCartney songs.
- March 1969: Dick James and Charles Silver sold their stake to Lew Grade's ATV without telling John or Paul. The news broke March 28, 1969. Paul was on his honeymoon (married Linda March 12; Daily Mirror placed him in the Bahamas/US); John had married Yoko March 20 and was at the Amsterdam Bed-In (Paul McCartney Project; Wikipedia).
- Dick James, Daily Mirror, March 29, 1969: "To telephone them would have been difficult."
- Dick James, Daily Mirror, April 5, 1969: "I really thought I was acting for them."
- By late 1969 ATV controlled Northern Songs. Lennon and McCartney kept their writer's income (Wikipedia).

## 1981: Paul and Yoko try to buy it back
- The Times (London), December 1981 (Paul McCartney Project transcript): Lord Grade "recently came close to accepting McCartney's bid of around £21m but backed away when various entertainment multinationals scented the deal". Paul: "He should not screw me for what he could get from somebody else. I'm not interested in buying his whole company. I just want my songs. Give me back my babies, Lew!"
- Wikipedia (Northern Songs) says the £21m bid was made with Yoko.
- John Lennon was killed Dec 8, 1980 in New York (said once, straight).
- 1982: Robert Holmes à Court took control of Grade's company ACC. 1984: ATV Music (about 4,000 songs, 251 Beatles songs) put up for sale.

## 1984-85: the auction
- Paul's side: felt buying John's half alone would look like a "Paul McCartney grab", wanted Yoko to split it; she declined (Pop History Dig). Paul's lawyer told Branca it was "too pricey" (Taraborrelli via Wikipedia; Forbes says "much too pricey").
- Bert Reuter, negotiator for Holmes à Court, Today Tonight (Australian TV), June 25, 2009: "We had given Paul McCartney first right of refusal but Paul didn't want it at that time."
- Yoko, November 1990 (Taraborrelli, via Wikipedia): "If Paul got the songs, people would have said, 'Paul finally got John.' And if I got them, they'd say, 'Oh, the dragon lady strikes again.'" She also called it a "blessing."
- Yoko to Branca (Forbes/Greenburg): "Oh, that would be wonderful in the hands of Michael rather than some big corporation." (not used)
- Branca's first purchase test (Forbes/Greenburg): two songs from songwriter Ernie Maresca. Michael: "You gotta get those songs! I danced to them all weekend."
- Early catalogs Michael bought: Sly Stone, Len Barry, the Soul Survivors, Dion's 1961 songs (Pop History Dig).
- September 1984, Victory tour: Branca tells Michael. TheWrap: "It includes a few things you might be interested in." Michael (Forbes): "You gotta get me that catalogue." "You can't put a price on a Picasso." "They're the best songs that have ever been written."
- Advisers against it: Forbes names Walter Yetnikoff as telling him to stick to being an artist (paraphrase). Script keeps it general.
- Nov 20, 1984: telex bid, $46 million (Hilburn, LA Times 1985, via Wikipedia; TheWrap).
- Eight contract drafts (TheWrap). About $1 million on due diligence; close to a million pages of contracts; four months (Forbes; Pop History Dig).
- Holmes à Court (Forbes/Greenburg; TheWrap): "The Viet Cong didn't play by the rules, and look what happened."
- Michael's note to Branca (Forbes): "John please let's not bargain... I don't want to lose the deal... IT'S MY CATALOGUE." Script reads it as "John please let's not bargain. I don't want to lose the deal." then "IT'S MY CATALOGUE."
- May 1985: Michael's team walked away. June 1985: Bandier and Koppelman (The Entertainment Co.) tentative deal at about $50 million, financed by MCA (Irving Azoff). Branca got Azoff to pull the money; his line to Azoff (Forbes): "How can you fund Charles and Marty? They're bidding against Michael and you're the consultant." Script paraphrases this.
- Concorde: Branca met Bandier and Koppelman on the same Concorde to London (Forbes excerpt, page 2). The "pretending to read" bit is Duck's joke, not a fact.
- Bandier raised by $500,000; Holmes à Court: "I'm selling this to Michael Jackson." (Forbes). TheWrap's Bandier version: "He said I've made a decision. I'm selling it to Michael Jackson."
- Bandier (TheWrap): "Charles and I couldn't do the moonwalk."
- Conditions: Michael appears on the Channel Seven Perth Telethon (Holmes à Court owned Channel Seven); a scholarship in Michael's name; "Penny Lane" excluded and given to Holmes à Court's teenage daughter (Wikipedia citing The Guardian 2016 and DW 2016; Forbes; TheWrap). Michael's company kept administering it for her (Forbes).
  - Name conflict: Wikipedia says the daughter was Catherine; TheWrap and Forbes call her "Penny". Script just says "his teenage daughter".
- Deal closed Aug 10, 1985, $47.5 million (Hilburn via Wikipedia; TheWrap).
- Michael gave Branca a Rolls-Royce (TheWrap).
- Perth, October 1985 (Midwest Times, Oct 2025): short live interview with Molly Meldrum and Peter Waltham, met two Little Telethon Stars, did not perform "due to contractual reasons". Jeff Newman: "Michael was very shy. I can still remember him being quite unfirm with his handshake." Flight length (about 15 hours LA to Sydney plus 5 to Perth) from Forbes; script says "twenty hours of flying".
- Possible extra, not used: Pop History Dig says Holmes à Court at one point suspected Michael was a front for Paul. Single aggregator source.

## After: just business
- Paul kept his writer's share; Michael owned the publisher's side (basic publishing split; Wikipedia Northern Songs).
- New York Post, Oct 2001 (via ABC News, Oct 22, 2001): Paul wrote letters; Michael's reply "Oh Paul, that's just business." Paul: "He won't even answer my letters, so we haven't talked and we don't have that great a relationship." And: "The trouble is I wrote those songs for nothing and buying them back at these phenomenal sums ... I just can't do it."
- Michael, per Taraborrelli (Michael Jackson: The Magic and the Madness, 2004 ed., pp. 335-338, via Wikipedia): "If he didn't want to invest $47.5 million in his own songs, then he shouldn't come crying to me now."
- Taraborrelli: Paul's net worth then about $560 million, royalty income $41 million; Paul had used the Buddy Holly catalog in commercials.

## Nike "Revolution"
- Debuted March 26, 1987, on NBC during The Cosby Show (Magnet, 2021).
- USA Today, May 25, 1988 (via Magnet): Nike paid $250,000 to Michael for publishing and $250,000 to EMI-Capitol for the master.
- Yoko, San Francisco Chronicle, Sept 18, 1987 (Ben Fong-Torres, via Magnet): "Michael Jackson was very considerate. He said no to 50 offers until this one, which seemed tasteful enough."
- July 28, 1987: Apple sued Nike, Wieden & Kennedy and Capitol-EMI for $15 million (Magnet; Pop History Dig). Not Michael.
- Apple statement (Pop History Dig): "The Beatles position is that they don't sing jingles to peddle sneakers, beer, pantyhose or anything else."
- George Harrison, November 1987 (Pop History Dig): "If it's allowed to happen, every Beatles song ever recorded is going to be advertising women's underwear and sausages." Wording varies a bit across retellings (another version has hot dogs and brassieres); this is the most widely cited.
- Paul, February 1988 (Pop History Dig): "Generally, I don't like it, particularly with the Beatles stuff."
- Nike stopped the ads by March 1988; settled November 1989, terms confidential (Pop History Dig).
- Michael recorded "Come Together" (1986), released on HIStory (1995) (Wikipedia).

## Sony and after
- 1995: ATV merged with Sony's publishing into Sony/ATV; Michael got a reported $95 million to $115 million for half (figures vary: $95M NPR/Ultimate Classic Rock, $110M Wikipedia/Guest, about $100M Pop History Dig). Script: "a reported hundred million or so". Deal sealed during a Tokyo concert (Wikipedia citing Guest 2006); script frames it "the story goes".
- Michael died June 25, 2009. Estate sold its half to Sony for about $750 million in 2016 (NPR; Wikipedia).
- Marty Bandier named chairman and CEO of Sony/ATV, effective April 1, 2007 (Sony press release, Feb 27, 2007); stepped down 2019 (Wikipedia). So he ran the company during Paul's 2017 suit.
- John Branca: later co-executor of Michael's estate.
- Paul: UK's wealthiest musician since the Sunday Times Rich List began in 1989 (Wikipedia, citing BBC 2024).

## Getting the songs back
- U.S. Copyright Act of 1976: authors can terminate transfers of pre-1978 works after 56 years (Wikipedia; Fortune).
- Paul served notices from as early as October 2008 (CNBC).
- Duran Duran lost a UK case to a Sony/ATV company in December 2016 over the same kind of reclaim (CNBC).
- Jan 18, 2017: McCartney v. Sony/ATV, S.D.N.Y., No. 17-00363; more than 260 copyrights; first, "Love Me Do", Oct 5, 2018 (Fortune/Reuters).
- Sony/ATV: suit "both unnecessary and premature"; "Sony/ATV has the highest respect for Sir Paul McCartney" (CNBC; Music Business Worldwide).
- Settled June 2017, confidential (Fortune/Reuters June 30, 2017). Exactly what Paul got is not public, so the script doesn't claim more than the law and the dismissal.

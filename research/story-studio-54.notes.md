# Notes: story-studio-54

Due To a Strict Door Policy / Who Kept Chic Out of Studio 54? Written Nov 27, 2026 from the Nov 25 idea note. Pairs with Disco Demolition (that script teases how "Le Freak" got written; this one pays it off and ends on the Comiskey callback).

Research method: WebSearch plus page downloads (curl) of the articles below, read from the full page text. nytimes.com and web.archive.org could not be fetched in this session, so the New York Times period articles are listed by headline/URL (taken from Wikipedia's footnotes) and their facts were cross-checked against Wikipedia and other coverage, not read directly. No quote in the script comes from an unread NYT page.

Framing rules followed (Duck, Nov 25): rock vs disco only, no race or sexuality angle; drugs mentioned straight, never the joke; Rubell's death one straight line; no Hamilton Jordan / White House cocaine story (political), no mob-ties material about Schrager's father (allegation-heavy, off the music).

## The back door / "Le Freak" (all Nile Rodgers's own tellings)
- Billboard, "Nile Rodgers To Be Inducted Into Songwriters Hall of Fame, Remembers 'Le Freak' & More", June 1, 2016: https://www.billboard.com/articles/news/features/7392739/nile-rodgers-songwriters-hall-fame-le-freak . Read in full. Quotes used exactly: Grace Jones "told us to come to the back door of Studio 54 and tell them that we were personal friends. We'd seen all the stuff about them separating the M&Ms ... Bella Lugosi and Marlene Dietrich, but the guy slammed the door in our faces and told us, 'Ah, fuck off.'" Second knock: "I told you, Ah fuck off." Dom Perignon "which we used to call rock'n'roll mouthwash". "Bernard, we can't get a song on the radio that says fuck off". French listeners hear "Africa". "The record was the biggest-selling record in Atlantic history. It held that title for 33 years". (Rodgers also claims they "cut off our sales at 7 million"; left out of the script.)
- Sound On Sound, Richard Buskin, "Classic Tracks: Chic 'Le Freak'", April 2005: https://www.soundonsound.com/node/4914423 . Read in full. "On New Year's Eve, 1977, we were invited to meet with Grace Jones at Studio 54"; "Grace Jones didn't leave our name at the door and the doorman wouldn't let us in"; apartment one block away; champagne from the corner liquor store; wanted a riff song like Cream's "Sunshine Of Your Love"; "our suits must have cost us a couple of thousand bucks each, and our really fancy shoes had got soaked trudging through the snow"; "So 'Fuck Off' was a protest song, and we actually thought it was pretty good ... 'Aaaaahh, fuck off!' It had a vibe. I was thinking 'This could be the anthem of everybody who gets cut off on the street by a cab driver or any kids who want to say this to their parents.'"; "We really had pretty big designs on completing the song as 'Fuck Off'"; "freak off ... that was pretty hideous"; "For one second the light bulb went on and I sang 'Aaaaahh, freak out!'". Also: Bernard Edwards died of pneumonia hours after a tribute show for Rodgers at Tokyo's Budokan, April 18, 1996.
- Stereogum, Tom Breihan, "The Number Ones: Chic's 'Le Freak'", Jan 15, 2020: https://stereogum.com/2069711/the-number-ones-chics-le-freak/columns/the-number-ones . Grace Jones was the headlining act at Studio 54 that night and forgot to leave their names; hit No. 1 Dec 9, 1978, six weeks total; knocked out of No. 1 twice and came back twice; Atlantic's best-selling single for more than a decade.
- Supporting retellings (same story, no new quotes used): American Songwriter, Far Out, Radio X.
- Dahl link: Disco Demolition research notes already show "Le Freak" at No. 1 on the Hot 100 charts dated Dec 23 and Dec 30, 1978, the week Steve Dahl was fired (Christmas Eve 1978). Used in a FILL in OUT.

## The club and the building
- Wikipedia, "Studio 54" (read the wikitext in full, footnotes checked): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Studio_54 . Gallo Opera House opened 1927, went bankrupt; CBS Studio 52 hosted What's My Line?, Password, Captain Kangaroo; Broadway Catering Corp. spent $400,000 on the conversion; Broadway lighting designers Jules Fisher and Paul Marantz; opened April 26, 1977; space for 2,500, about 4,000 attended, invited celebrities stuck outside; admission generally $7 or $8; New York magazine, Nov 1977, Rubell: "I turned away 1,400 people last Saturday."; president of Cyprus rejected because doormen thought he was president of Cypress Hills Cemetery; Saudi embassy letter after one of King Khalid's sons was rejected; guests pulled guns, security cleared trash cans for blocks; $1.2 million renovation in late 1979 "in anticipation of increasing interest in rock music"; Valentine's Day 1979 party with 3,000 Dutch tulips and 4,000 sq yd of sod from Bermuda; June 1979 indictment ($2.5 million, as much as 60 percent of receipts, citing NYT June 29, 1979); guilty pleas Nov 1979; 3.5 years each Jan 1980 (NYT Jan 19, 1980); final party night of Feb 2 to 3, 1980 with Diana Ross and Liza Minnelli singing; reported to prison two days later; liquor license not renewed Feb 28, lost Feb 29, 1980, fruit punch the next day.
- Wikipedia, "Steve Rubell": https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Rubell . Met Schrager at Syracuse; Steak Loft restaurants; $7 million first year; Rubell's own name for the system: "cash-in, cash-out and skim"; police reports say cash and receipts hidden in the ceiling sections of Rubell's office; sentenced Jan 18, 1980 to 3.5 years and $20,000 fines each; released April 17, 1981; Morgans hotel; died July 25, 1989.
- WWD interview, Rosemary Feitelberg, "Forty Minutes With Studio 54 Cofounder Ian Schrager", March 11, 2020 (read via Yahoo syndication): https://www.yahoo.com/lifestyle/forty-minutes-studio-54-cofounder-191233939.html . Quotes used exactly: man in the moon "Nothing could be further from the truth."; Cher on the Post front page in overalls and a straw hat; Steve called at 5 a.m.: "We made it"; door "was like creating a salad. Steve was very, very good at it."; "I've seen wives leave their husbands to get in and couples split up. I'm serious."; Bianca's party on a dark Monday, "We had two nude people with clothes [body]-painted on them. The woman came out with long blonde hair, pulling a birthday cake. She got off the horse and Bianca jumped on, in a red dress on a white horse." (source has an em dash before "in a red dress"; read as a comma); "We weren't some organized gang. We were the gang that couldn't shoot straight. All this money was coming in and it was the worst kept secret in town."; he recalls the New York magazine quote "that we made more money than the mafia but don't tell anybody"; on cash in the ceiling: "No, not true, folklore. But there were equally silly places. I had money stashed in the trunk of my car."; served 13 months.
- Hollywood Reporter review of the documentary "Studio 54" (Matt Tyrnauer, 2018): https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/review/studio-54-1077207 . The catering-permit dodge; a lawman in the film says the raid came from a disgruntled employee's tip, not the bragging; the owners cooperated against other club owners.

## The money and the raid
- Grazia, Michael Kaplan, "Disco Decadence and How Studio 54 Came Crashing Down", July 1, 2021 (republished Sept 8, 2022): https://graziamagazine.com/us/articles/disco-decadence-and-how-studio-54-came-crashing-down/ . Read in full. Interviews with prosecutor Peter Sudler (Asst. U.S. Attorney, SDNY), employee Scott Nilsson, Myra Scheer, Marc Benecke. Used: Rubell to Dan Dorfman, fall 1977: "The profits [are] astronomical. Only the mafia does it better." and "It's a cash business and you have to worry about the IRS. I don't want them to know everything." (Wikipedia: Dorfman wrote for New York magazine, Nov 1977; Grazia gives the wording; original column not seen.) Nilsson: "They would close the register and pull out the money. The first set of tapes was reported and the second set was not." Tipster Donald Moon; Sudler: "He was angry about being passed over for a promotion."; Moon alleged a second set of books "kept in the ceiling tiles". Dec 14, 1978, "a reported 31 agents", door opened by somebody, "probably a 'cleaning man'". Sudler on Schrager walking in: "I thought it was incredibly stupid." Manila envelopes "for every day"; "It was almost 80 percent. Typically, you don't skim that much." Records had a column with the word "skim" (per the documentary). Warhol's 50th: Rubell dumped 800 $1 bills over his head, later booked as a Warhol expense; Warhol to New York magazine: "Why would Steve [Rubell] do that? … No wonder people are afraid to go there now." ## Not verified / left out on purpose
- The exact wording of Rubell's New York magazine "Mafia" line: Wikipedia paraphrases "only the Mafia made more money"; Schrager remembers "we made more money than the mafia but don't tell anybody"; the script reads Grazia's wording and says Grazia quoted it.
- Cash in the ceiling: the tipster alleged it (Sudler), Wikipedia cites police reports of cash and receipts in the ceiling of Rubell's office, Grazia says more than $3 million was seized from the ceiling, a bank box and home safes; Schrager calls the ceiling "folklore". Script plays it as Legend or Fact, "depends who you ask". The "$600,000 in garbage bags" figure (All That's Interesting) has no primary source and isn't used.
- Sudler's "gray people" anecdote: sources disagree on whether Rubell or Schrager said it, so it's out.
- The man who died in a ventilation shaft trying to get in (Haden-Guest; Schrager confirmed per Wikipedia): left out; a death, and it would be a downer in the door section.
- Who the doorman was on New Year's Eve 1977: nobody names him; the script doesn't either.
- Celebrity-gossip claims without a named source (frostbite story, Margaret Trudeau photo, etc.) left out.

## For Grok / Defa: where the show stands (updated Nov 16 by Claude, at Dillon's request)
Grok and Defa: read this before doing anything for the show. The full rules live in `CLAUDE.md` ("The show" section); this is the short version plus what changed Nov 14 to 16.

**What the show is**
- Comedy podcast, solo host **Duck** (Chicagoland, 32 years playing guitar). Crazy or really funny true stories from music history, told Dollop style. **Never mention his tribute band, Counterfeit, or "my band" anywhere.** No em dashes in any copy.
- Titles: "Due To ..." (like a cancellation notice) + a "Who ...?" subtitle that points at the guilty party.
- **How every episode opens (Duck, Nov 16):** short teaser, then "I'm Duck, and due to [title], this is Defacing the Music." (e.g. "I'm Duck, and due to a possible rabies infection, this is Defacing the Music."), then the theme song, then "Episode 1. Who Let That Guy Bring a Bat?" and into the story. Title before the theme, subtitle after it. Every episode names **That Guy** (the one to blame), marked by a short guitar squeal Duck records himself.
- YouTube is the main platform. Also on Spotify and Apple Podcasts. Socials: Facebook, Instagram, TikTok (links on defacingthemusic.com).
- Old promo line "Fifteen minutes. No refunds." is retired: episodes run 15 to 30 minutes now, and Duck didn't like "no refunds".

**The vibe (Duck's top rule)**
- **The Dollop and How Did This Get Made.** Two pillars every episode: (1) "I went down the rabbit hole for you": oddly specific details about what one person said, did or saw; (2) the decisions behind the disaster: who thought this was a good idea, and why.
- **It's read, like the Dollop** (Duck, Nov 16): "it's clear that david anthony reads, and it isnt off the top of the head." Scripts are full researched stories Duck reads, with real quotes read straight from named sources. Nothing is made up; unproven stuff is called a legend.
- People who got hurt or died are never the joke.
- **HARD RULE (Duck, Nov 19): no political debates, racial topics or other polarizing subjects in any episode.** Not in scripts, TALK cues, jokes or show copy. If a story touches one, state the fact briefly and move on, with no debate and no take. Applies to every episode, the library scripts and future ones.
- **Read longer passages** (Duck, Nov 17): a few full sentences straight from a news article or magazine is good; scripts were trying too hard to summarize everything.
- **More real quotes** (Duck, Nov 17): scripts are missing real quotes read from named sources. Casual off-the-cuff lines are fine (Duck puts them in his own words); jokes stay.

### Nov 22, Duck: CUT on a pause = too long, decide by context
**A `CUT` on a pause means "too long", not "zero" (Duck, Nov 22).** Duck marked many pauses `CUT` before `SHORTEN` existed (most of the first half hour of episode 1) and won't go back. So for any `CUT >> PAUSE` (or a cut gap between lines), decide by context: if the pause comes right after a punchline, a reveal, a big fact or a laugh, or right before a reveal, or Pat might react in it, shorten it to a natural beat (about half a second to a second) instead of removing it. Remove it fully only when it's plainly dead air (lost place, fumbling, waiting) and the sentences join smoothly without it. `SHORTEN` always means keep a beat. When unsure, keep a short beat; a gap that's slightly long beats a rushed punchline. (Also in docs/EDITING-KICKOFF.md.)

### Nov 22, Duck: keep the pauses
**Don't cut pauses by default (Duck, Nov 22).** A lot of pauses are on purpose: a beat after a point lands, room for Pat to react, a breath before a reveal. Keep them. Only cut a pause if I marked it, or if it's obviously dead air (lost my place, fumbling, a technical stop, waiting for something). The `>> PAUSE` flags in the transcript are information, not orders. Never trim the silence right after a punchline, a reveal or a laugh. (Also in docs/EDITING-BRIEF.md and docs/EDITING-KICKOFF.md; this replaces the brief's old "remove long audible pauses".)

### Nov 22, Duck: don't over-edit; small cuts are optional
Duck marks lots of single words and tiny bits.

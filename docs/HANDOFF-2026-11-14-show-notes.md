## 1. Decisions and rules Duck stated (these are his calls)

### Story selection rule (applies to every episode)
- Every episode must be EITHER a story everybody knows told with new information, OR a story people don't really know.
- Duck's words: "I want people to listen to an episode, and even if they know the story, get something new or fresh or interesting that then makes them want to listen to the other stories. Like, oh yeah, I knew Ozzy bit the head off a bat, but I wonder what else is with that story."
- "New information" means deeper details: how it got handled, how it affected people's personal lives, how it really played out. The basic story alone is not enough.
- The show is NOT about things merely going wrong. Duck: "It's about crazy things or really funny parts of crazy stories." A story that's just "something went badly" (example: Dylan going electric) doesn't qualify unless there's something crazy or funny in it.

### Legends rule
- Legends are fine IF they're called legends and the episode plays up how wild the retellings got. Duck: "the legends do work, as long as it's treated like that and we highlight how crazy the stories about it got."
- A "how the legend grew" episode is a valid format (example: Keith Moon's car in the pool).

### Episode length (overrides earlier "15 minutes" guidance)
- 15 minutes is the MINIMUM. Closer to 30 is probably ideal.
- Scripts that run 16+ minutes before riffing are "totally cool." Do NOT trim scripts for length.
- Reason: Duck doesn't know yet how good he'll be at riffing, so the script alone must hit the minimum.
- He'll edit down later once he figures out recording. Right now it's a single camera on him, one take, with no natural way to cut without it looking odd.

### Episode 0 is published
- Episode 0 is done and out there. It can't be changed. Any fix that touches ep 0 has to happen in later episodes instead.

### Episode 1 change
- CUT the "POP QUIZ ANSWER" opening line "Last episode I left you with a question...". Duck: the question he actually left people with "was kind of dumb," nobody would know the answer or be familiar with it, and it didn't add anything. Rework the section so the Herman's Hermits reveal stands on its own without pointing back to ep 0.

### Show format (Duck's description, confirmed)
1. Cold open: a wild, brief teaser, just a few sentences about what the episode's about.
2. "I'm Duck, and this is Defacing the Music."
3. Theme song.
4. Coming out of the theme: Duck starts the episode, announces the title, and gets into it.
5. Sign-off: something like "I'm Duck. Due to unforeseen circumstances, I'll see you next week." Then back into the theme song and done.

### Recurring bit: "That Guy"
- Every story has a point where Duck names "That Guy," the one who caused it or the one to blame. "Because in every story there's a 'that guy.' Don't be that guy."
- Pairs with the "Who ...?" subtitle, which basically names the suspect.

### "That Guy" sound sting
- Duck's worry: sound effects can sound hokey and cheesy.
- Agreed approach: ONE signature sting, used the same way every time. Avoid stock effects (air horns, laugh tracks, cartoon boings).
- Duck liked the idea of a guitar pinch-harmonic squeal. He'll record it himself on his own guitar (sounds less hokey than a library sound).
- Guidance: about one second, short and ugly. Record a few versions with different notes and gain, and pick the one that makes him laugh. Too long or too clean starts to feel like a bit.
- Duck remembered Aqua Teen Hunger Force doing something like this (unconfirmed, just his reference for the vibe).

### Optional segments (not every episode)
- **Legend or Fact:** near the end, call out which parts are real and which grew over the years. Duck: good, but NOT every episode, only when a story has those questions.
- **Where Are They Now:** a closer on what happened to the people involved afterward. Duck: "a very good idea," and a good way to add content and length to an episode.

### Social clips (important to Duck)
- Every episode needs moments that can be cut for TikTok, Shorts and Reels. Things people can consume quickly to get hooked and get the vibe without listening to a whole episode.
- The "That Guy" moment is a natural self-contained clip. Write scripts so there are several 30 to 60 second standalone moments per episode.

### Listener submissions (later, if the audience grows)
- If enough listeners send in stories, do special episodes, about half an hour, where Duck reads submitted stories.
- Use a slightly different title: "Defacing the Music: [something]". Duck wanted something more than "Listener Submissions" and floated a local or small-band angle.
- Options discussed: "Local Opener" (Claude's top pick, the no-name band that plays first), "Dive Bar Edition," "Basement Show," "Open Mic Night," "Battle of the Bands." Also floated earlier in an official-notice voice: "Incident Reports," "Complaints Department." Nothing chosen yet.

### Recording order (suggested, Duck didn't lock it)
- Record eps 1 to 3 as planned.
- Then Disco Demolition, then the Dave Matthews bus: two Chicago stories early, easiest for Duck to riff on.

- "A whole office full of guys" appears in 9 of 13 scripts. Save it for when it's genuinely a group.
- `[Pause.]` is used 10 to 15 times per script. At that density it stops doing anything. Fewer, better-placed pauses.
- Repeated phrases: "I looked this up twice," "I need you to picture," "Honesty check," "Remember that name / hold onto that."

# Growth playbook: what to do, watch for and avoid (Duck, Nov 23, 2026)

Duck: "make sure you are notating all this stuff, so its applied to everything moving forward, like things to do, be aware of, avoid etc." Every Claude session reads this before planning content, Shorts, posting, sponsors or anything that touches the channel. Add to it whenever Duck decides something new or we learn something from the numbers.

## The goal and the honest timeline
- **Target: about 5,000 listens per episode** (audio downloads + video views, counted in the first 30 days). That's where advertisers start taking a show seriously. It puts a show in roughly the top few percent of all podcasts, so it's a milestone, not a starting point.
- **Realistic pace** if something posts every week (a full episode or a side story) plus 3 to 5 Shorts a week: about 100 to 500 per episode at 3 months, 500 to 1,500 at 6 months, 1,500 to 5,000 at 12 months; **5,000 most likely in 12 to 18 months.** One Short that takes off can jump that by months. Set expectations by this, not by hype.
- Most shows that would have made it quit around month 4 to 6. Consistency is the plan.

## Length math (learned from episodes 1 and 2)
- Pat's reactions and riffing roughly **double** the reading time. A 20 to 30 minute script became an hour-long episode that couldn't be cut shorter.
- So write to the finished length you want, halved: Off The Record is 3 stories in about 30 minutes, so 2 to 3 minutes of reading per story. Keep main episodes at the low end of 20 to 30 minutes of reading if the goal is under an hour.

## What moves the numbers (in order)
1. **A steady schedule: something every week.** Full episodes take weeks of research, so fill the gaps with side stories (Off The Record, Mic Swap, Encore, and a Fresh Tracks when something wild happens). Monthly-only posting makes growth crawl.
2. **Win the first second of every Short.** The first Short (Ozzy bat, 21s) kept only 30% past the first second ("stayed to watch"); Shorts that keep getting pushed sit around 60 to 70%+. Rules in `docs/EDITING-BRIEF.md` ("Shorts, Reels, TikTok"): open on the craziest specific line, the part people don't know (the dove, the frog, the cow's head, not "the bat"), big hook text and captions from frame one, no lead-in, 20 to 45 seconds, end on a laugh, then the 2-second end card (`brand/shorts/`).
3. **Digestible sizes, not just long episodes** (Duck, Nov 23: the first two episodes run about an hour and won't cut shorter). Three sizes per story: Shorts (20 to 45 seconds), **segments** (5 to 15 minute standalone chapters on YouTube, 2 to 4 per episode, rules in `docs/EDITING-BRIEF.md`), and the full episode with YouTube chapters. Plus side stories that are short by design: Off The Record (3 quick stories, about 30 minutes) and Fresh Tracks (one wild news story, solo, a few minutes, only when something big happens). New people sample a segment before they commit an hour.
4. **Volume of Shorts:** several per episode, one every day or two around a release. Each one is a fresh test.
5. **Guest swaps with similar podcasts** (Mic Swap format): go on theirs, they come on ours.
6. **Hometown press for Disco Demolition** (Chicago outlets love that story).
7. **Post where the fans already are:** Reddit and Facebook groups for the bands, classic rock, metal, music history. Only when the episode is genuinely their story; never spam.
8. **Evergreen, searchable topics** (Ozzy's bat, Woodstock '99, Disco Demolition) keep getting found for years. Titles and descriptions should use the words people search.

## Reading YouTube numbers (so nobody panics or celebrates early)
- **Views vs engaged views:** since 2025 every Short start counts as a view. "Engaged views" (people who actually watched) is the real number. First Short: 171 views, 39 engaged (about 1 in 4), average 0:11 of 0:21.
- **A steep jump after a slow start** (around hour 3 on the first Short) means YouTube showed it to a bigger batch. Good sign. Check again after a day: still climbing = still being pushed; flat = done, improve the next hook.
- **"Stayed to watch vs swiped away"** is the number to improve on every Short.
- **Demographics (age/gender) mean nothing until a few thousand views.** YouTube only counts signed-in viewers with age set and hides small groups, so with tiny numbers one 55-to-64-year-old man shows as "100%". Ignore it early.
- **Unique viewers shows 0** for the first day or two. It fills in late.
- When Duck shares analytics, tell him what the number actually means, what to change in the next Short, and when to check again.

## What the numbers have taught us so far
- **Timely, searchable topics work (Nov 24):** the GTA 6 Fresh Track ("Who owns GTA 6?", 1:25) hit 1,110 views in its first day, about double the Ozzy Short, plus the channel's first new subscriber. **83% came from the Shorts feed** (a burst of up to ~300 views an hour in the first few hours, then it faded, the normal Shorts pattern); **YouTube search brought 123 and became the main source once the feed push ended** (people searching the GTA 6 copyright mess). So: a strong hook on a hot topic earns the feed push, and a searchable topic keeps it finding people afterward. When a big, searchable thing happens with a real music angle, do a quick Fresh Track fast.
- **News Shorts are the front door; episodes are the house (Nov 24, Duck: "the good videos so far were not episode related, but maybe boosted an episode related one"):** the GTA 6 Fresh Track (1,400 views) beat the Ozzy episode Short (525), and the episode clips picked up speed after it (the next episode clip had the channel's fastest start). Plan: timely Fresh Tracks pull new people in; episode clips, segments and full episodes keep them.
- **Always set "Related video" on every Short** (Short details page, bottom right; it was "None" on all of them on Nov 24): point it at the full episode it came from, and point Fresh Tracks at the newest full episode. It's a tappable link on the Short and turns Shorts viewers into long-form watch time, which is what counts toward Partner Program hours.
- **Shorts get second waves (Nov 24):** the GTA 6 Short went flat after about 5 hours, then about 14 hours after posting it spiked again (187 views in an hour, 151 from the Shorts feed) as YouTube re-tested it on a new batch. Never call a Short dead after one flat night, and don't change its title or thumbnail while a wave is running. Duck had posted an unrelated Short about 15 minutes before the spike, so a new upload may wake up the channel's recent Shorts (the views still came from the Shorts feed, not the channel page). Watch for it after the next few posts to confirm; if it holds, it's one more reason to post often.
- **Length matters (Nov 24):** the GTA 6 Short ran 1:25 and was watched about 39 seconds on average (about half); the feed push stopped after about 5 hours. Thumbnail click-through was a solid 7.6%, so the packaging worked; the length didn't. Rule since: Shorts about 60 seconds (about 150 words), no longer.
- **Funny clips reach, story Shorts convert (Nov 24, four Shorts side by side):** the 0:25 Duck and Pat clip ("This is how Defacing The Music st...") was the fastest starter ever (1,100 views in 4 hours) but earned 0 subscribers. It kept 58.8% past the first second (the first Short kept 30%), so the opening-line rule works; about 60% is the bar to keep clearing. Slayer kept **71%** past the first second (best yet) but was watched only about 22 of its 71 seconds on average (3.1 watch hours over 499 views): the hook works, the middle loses people. Keep Fresh Tracks near 60 seconds and keep a new specific detail coming every few lines. The 1:11 Slayer Fresh Track got 499 in 3 hours and +3 subscribers, the best rate so far. GTA 6 (1,400 views, 1:25) got 1; the 0:20 Ozzy bat clip (551 in a day and a half) got 0. Lesson: short funny host moments win the feed, but a real story is what makes people follow. Keep both in the mix; judge Shorts by subscribers and "stayed to watch", not just views. Two Shorts posted the same day both climbed at once, so they didn't steal from each other.
- **Long videos turn viewers into subscribers (Nov 25, Views by Content):** Episode 0 got 3 subscribers from just 70 views (about 4%), as many as Slayer got from 855; the GTA Short got 1 from 1,655 and the 0:25 Duck and Pat clip 0 from 1,218. Episode 1 people watch about 11 minutes each (19 views, 3.4 hours). So the job of every Short is to send people to a full episode: Related video set to the episode, a pinned comment with the link, and an end screen on every long video pointing to the next one. Shorts are the advertising; episodes are where people decide to stay.
- **The Earn page lags (Nov 25):** its eligibility numbers say "Showing data as of" a date several days back (Nov 20 when checked Nov 25: 161 qualified Shorts views, 1 watch hour, before the GTA, Slayer and Duck and Pat Shorts took off). Check that date before worrying. Qualified watch hours only ever count long-form (episodes, segments, Off The Record); the ~35 watch hours in Studio are mostly Shorts, which never count toward the 3,000.
- **Full episodes: win the first 3 minutes (Nov 27, Ep 1 retention, 53:34 long, 11:04 average, 20.7% watched):** about half the viewers left in the first 30 seconds, the line slid to about 20% by minute 3, and then it stayed almost flat all the way to the end. People who get past minute 3 stay for the whole hour, so every bit of effort on retention goes into the first 3 minutes. What was there: the cold open, the theme, then "Let's start at the beginning" and Ozzy's birth year. Fixes: (1) open the video on the strongest moment, not a slow frame; (2) keep the theme short in the video; (3) right after "Episode N. Who ...?", state the crazy thing plainly and promise the part people don't know ("that's not even the crazy part"), before any backstory; (4) never start a section after the theme with a birth date or "let's start at the beginning"; (5) put the names people search in the YouTube title next to the "Due To" title (e.g. "Ozzy Bit a Bat. Here's Why. | Ep 1: Due To a Possible Rabies Infection"), so the people who click are the ones who wanted this story. The early drop is partly people who were never going to commit to an hour; segments and Shorts are for them.
- **Shorts watch time doesn't count toward Partner Program hours.** The 3,000 (or 4,000) watch hours only come from long-form videos: full episodes, segments, Off The Record. Shorts count only on the separate "qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days" path. So the hours come from getting people to watch the long stuff; segments help because they're long-form.

## Money: what to turn on and when
- **Now (no audience needed):** affiliate links in descriptions for gear Duck actually uses (Sweetwater, Reverb, Guitar Center / Musician's Friend, Amazon). Spotify for Creators > **Monetize** page shows Partner Program eligibility (Spotify lowered the bar in January 2026; check the page for current numbers). Upload the video version to Spotify too.
- **YouTube Partner Program:** 1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours (or the Shorts views route).
- **Around 2,000 to 5,000 per episode:** pitch brands directly with the sponsor page (`/sponsor/`, admin > Sponsors). Small brands first; free product first is normal ("send us one to try, if we like it we'll talk about it on the show"); ask for cash once the numbers are there. Host-read ads pay much better than automatic ones.
- **Who to pitch:** list in admin > Sponsors (strings and picks, indie pedal builders, headphones/speakers like Marshall and Soundcore, tees and vinyl, learn-to-play apps, Liquid Death, Chicagoland shops). Only stuff Duck would actually use.
- **Ad marketplaces** (Spotify, Acast, RedCircle, AdvertiseCast, Podbean) keep roughly 30 to 50%; sponsor-matching sites (Podcorn, Gumball) roughly 20 to 30%; selling direct keeps 100%. An ad agency or network is worth its cut only when the show is big enough that they bring brands Duck can't reach.
- **Ballpark for the big shows** (ads/streaming only, guesses, not published numbers): Last Podcast on the Left $3M to $8M+ a year; Comedy Bang Bang and How Did This Get Made $1M to $4M; The Dollop $1M to $3M. Paid bonus feeds (Patreon) are often their biggest earner. Useful for motivation, not for planning.

## Things to avoid (learned the hard way)
- **Never use pictures Duck found online in an episode unless they're free to use.** His three episode 1 uploads ("Ozzy", "dove", "randy") were press photos (one by Ross Halfin, a pro rock photographer known for going after unlicensed use). A credit line doesn't make a photo legal. Only Wikimedia Commons CC BY / CC BY-SA / CC0 / public domain, or Duck's own photos. If a picture is already in a video that's about to go public, say so before it goes out.
- **Don't run a website login against Duck's Google account.** Connecting the site to Gmail (app passwords, made and deleted several times on a brand-new account, plus failed logins from the IONOS server) triggered a Google security hold on the podcast Gmail on Nov 23. For list email, use the IONOS mailbox on defacingthemusic.com or a newsletter service (MailerLite), not Gmail. If Google ever holds the account: stop all retries, wait a day or two, sign in once from the usual computer and Wi-Fi, use accounts.google.com/signin/recovery if needed.
- **No polarizing people or topics**, even when the story is great: the Marilyn Manson rib legend was swapped out (abuse allegations), and the Ye opera lawsuit was skipped for the same reason. Hard rule in CLAUDE.md.
- **No AI music controversy (Duck, Nov 25):** the theme song is AI-generated, so no episodes, Fresh Tracks or Off The Record stories about AI music (generators, AI content mills, AI copyright fights, voice clones). It would invite people to pick at the theme.
- **Nobody hurt or dead is the joke**, and fresh tragedies (a concert gassing, a building collapse) are not news Shorts.
- **"Side Stories" is the name of Last Podcast on the Left's bonus show.** Fine as an admin label; never use it as a public series name.
- **Shorts over 3 minutes stop counting as Shorts** on YouTube. Fresh Tracks videos run a few minutes; the Short cut from one keeps to the 3 minute limit.
- **Long end cards hurt the loop.** 2 seconds max, hard cut after the last laugh.
- **Don't publish raw video on the website.** IONOS caps real storage around 20 GB (shared with Duck's other sites) and the file manager has a 50 MB per-file limit. Raw files stay on Duck's PC; `/dtm-raw` sits outside the site.
- **Nothing posts anywhere without Duck's OK.**

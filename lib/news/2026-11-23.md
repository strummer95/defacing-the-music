## 1. StubHub sold a concert that doesn't exist
**Happened:** 2026-11-15 (NZ Herald story; the promoters heard about the listing the week before)
**Wild because:** a ticket site listed a whole concert nobody booked. **Why anyone cares:** everybody has bought tickets online and wondered if they're real.
**On-screen hook text:** They sold tickets to a concert that doesn't exist
**Title:** StubHub listed a concert that was never booked
**Sources:** [NZ Herald: Fans warned after ticket resale platform StubHub advertises non-existent Manukau Ten Tenors concert](https://www.nzherald.co.nz/entertainment/fans-warned-after-ticket-resale-platform-stubhub-advertises-non-existent-manukau-ten-tenors-concert/5VYZGFYLYBCGTOJ4CHOOZL4Y4A/)

StubHub advertised tickets to a concert that does not exist. Not sold out. Not cancelled. Never existed.

New Zealand. The Ten Tenors, which is exactly what it sounds like, ten guys singing very loud. StubHub had them playing March 1st at a venue in Manukau, south Auckland. The promoters found out from somebody else. The venue told the New Zealand Herald, quote, "We have not listed this event for sale." The promoters had to put out a statement just to say where their own show actually is: "The actual Auckland concert is at the Aotea Centre on February 28, 2027."

So the real show is a different venue, a different day, and the one on StubHub was imaginary. StubHub took it down, but the Herald says you could still find it on Google. The Commerce Commission has two complaints and says, "We will complete an assessment of these concerns."

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: whoever looked at a concert with no venue, no promoter and no band booked, and said, yeah, list it.

## 2. A nu-metal band played a Denny's
**Happened:** 2026-11-07
**Wild because:** a mosh-pit band played a family restaurant, with corporate's blessing. **Why anyone cares:** everybody's been to a Denny's at 2 a.m. Nobody's seen a pit there.
**On-screen hook text:** A metal band crashed Denny's corporate. Denny's said yes.
**Title:** Silly Goose talked Denny's into a metal show
**Sources:** [Theprp: Silly Goose Performed Live At A Denny's Yesterday And Shot A New Music Video](https://www.theprp.com/2026/11/08/news/silly-goose-performed-live-at-a-dennys-yesterday-and-shot-a-new-music-video/)

A nu-metal band talked Denny's into letting them play a Denny's. On purpose. With permission.

The band is called Silly Goose. Back in September, their singer, Jackson Foster, crashed a Denny's corporate conference to pitch it. Just walked in where the executives were and asked. And it worked. November 7th, a Denny's in Norcross, Georgia, a full metal show next to the Grand Slams, and they shot the music video for their song "Goodnight" right there.

Here's the part I love. This band normally plays guerrilla shows at fast-food places and gas stations. No permission. They just show up. This was the rare one where head office said yes.

And they're not even the first. Blink-182 played a Denny's in 2023. So Denny's has now hosted more rock shows than some actual venues.

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: the Denny's executive who heard "a metal band wants to play your restaurant" and said, what's the worst that could happen.

## 3. Fishermen went out for marlin and caught a pianist
**Happened:** 2026-11-06
**Wild because:** two guys fishing 20 miles out found a cruise ship pianist who'd been floating for nine hours. **Why anyone cares:** it's a survival story with a happy ending. **Careful:** he's alive and expected to be OK, but the Coast Guard is still investigating how he went over. Don't joke about how he fell, and hold this one if the cause turns out to be dark. The joke is the fishing trip, not him.
**On-screen hook text:** They went fishing for marlin. They caught a pianist.
**Title:** Fishermen found a cruise pianist after 9 hours in the ocean
**Sources:** [Fox 19 / Gray TV: Cruise ship pianist who fell overboard rescued from 'middle of the ocean' after 9 hours](https://www.fox19.com/2026/11/09/cruise-ship-pianist-who-fell-overboard-rescued-middle-ocean-after-9-hours/), [TravelPulse: Cruise ship pianist rescued after going overboard](https://www.travelpulse.com/news/cruise/cruise-ship-pianist-rescued-after-going-overboard-prompting-us-coast-guard-investigation)

Two guys went fishing for marlin off California and caught a piano player.

November 6th. Royal Caribbean's Ovation of the Seas is heading from Long Beach down to Ensenada, Mexico. Around 2:30 in the morning, the ship's pianist ends up in the Pacific. The ship keeps going. Nobody notices.

He floats out there for about nine hours. Then two fishermen out of Newport Beach, Sean Norton and Grant Garrett, are cruising around near Catalina Island looking for marlin, and they spot a guy. In the middle of the ocean. Almost 20 miles from shore. He tells them he's the piano player on the cruise ship.

They pull him in. The water was unusually warm that day, which probably saved his life. He went to the hospital, and the Coast Guard says he's expected to survive. They're still figuring out how he went over.

No That Guy on this one yet. Just two guys who went out for a big fish and came home with a better story than any fish.

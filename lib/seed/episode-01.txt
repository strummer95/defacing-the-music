## COLD OPEN
September 1967. A TV studio in Los Angeles. Tommy Smothers walks up to a British rock band with a microphone and a big smile, and asks them a few friendly questions.
About ninety seconds later, the drum kit explodes, the guitarist's hair is on fire, and Tommy Smothers is holding an acoustic guitar that is about to stop being a guitar.

Herman's Hermits.
The Who. "My Generation." Smash the guitar, kick over the amps, destroy the stage. Opening for Herman's Hermits. "Mrs. Brown, You've Got a Lovely Daughter." That's the tour.

## WHO IS KEITH MOON
Now, you need to understand who Keith Moon was, because "drummer" doesn't cover it.
Keith Moon was the guy who looked at a five star hotel room and thought, "Now how do I get this to look like a one star hotel room."
He blew up hotel toilets with cherry bombs. That's not a legend, that was a hobby. Hotels across America knew his name the way the post office knows a guy with a dog that bites.
[RIFF: your take on Keith Moon. "Life was a fuse burning down too slowly" if you want it.]
And here's a fun one, and this part is "the story goes," so take it with a grain of salt. On that same 1967 tour, Keith turned twenty one at a Holiday Inn in Flint, Michigan. There was a cake fight, there was a lot of damage, and the legend says a car ended up in the hotel pool. Did a car end up in the pool? Nobody has ever proven it. Did The Who get a very large bill from that Holiday Inn? Yes.
That's our guy. Now let's give him explosives.

## THE SETUP
The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour. CBS. Big Sunday night show. Tom and Dick Smothers did folk songs and comedy, and they were the cool show, the one that booked rock bands.
The Who come on to play "I Can See for Miles" and "My Generation." Their big finish was always destruction. Pete smashes the guitar, Keith kicks over the drums. For TV, they want it bigger. So the plan is a small explosive charge in Keith's bass drum. A little flash, a little bang, great television.
Here's where the story gets murky, and I'm going to be honest with you about it.
The common version is that Keith paid off a stagehand to load extra flash powder. Some accounts say the crew loaded it, and Keith added more on top when nobody was looking. One account says it ended up around ten times what it should have been. Nobody agrees on exactly who did what.
[RIFF: "there is always a guy" callback. Somewhere in that building is a stagehand who said "yeah, that's probably fine."]
What everybody agrees on is the result.

## THE BOOM
They play "My Generation." Pete's smashing his guitar. Keith kicks over his kit. And the charge goes off.
It is way too big. The blast blows Keith off his drum riser. A piece of cymbal cuts his arm. Pete Townshend is standing right in front of it, and it singes his hair.
[RIFF: describe the clip. Pete's face. The smoke. It's on YouTube, tell people to go watch it after.]
And Pete has said that night is part of why his hearing has been bad for the rest of his life. He's lived with tinnitus for decades. Now, to be fair, he also stood in front of the loudest amps on Earth for sixty years, so it's not ALL Keith's fault. But Pete has pointed at that night.
Here's my favorite detail. The smoke is clearing. Pete's hair is smoking. And Tommy Smothers walks out with an acoustic guitar, all smiles, ready to do a bit.
Pete grabs the guitar out of his hands and smashes it on the floor.
That's on video. That's real. The host of the show walked over to make a joke, and lost his guitar.
[RIFF: Tommy's reaction. "This is my guitar, I was going to play it later."]

## THE LEGEND
Now, one more piece of this story gets told everywhere, and I have to put it in the "the legend goes" pile.
The story goes that Bette Davis was backstage, waiting to go on, and she was so freaked out by the explosion that she fainted into the arms of Mickey Rooney.
Is that true? I can't prove it. It shows up in a lot of retellings. But I love it so much that I'm telling you anyway, with a giant asterisk.
[RIFF: picture it. Old Hollywood royalty, a cloud of smoke, a British drummer bleeding.]

## THE FALLOUT
So what happened to everybody?
Keith Moon got cut, got patched up, and went right back to being Keith Moon. If anything, it was great for business.
Pete Townshend got a story he's told for fifty years, and ears that ring.
And The Smothers Brothers? CBS canceled their show in 1969. Not because of The Who, they had a whole fight with the network over politics and censorship. But I like to think the drum kit didn't help.

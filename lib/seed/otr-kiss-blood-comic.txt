## KISS PRINTED THEIR BLOOD IN A COMIC BOOK
[CLIP: KISS blood comic]
KISS put their actual blood in a comic book. With a notary watching.

The legend: in 1977, Marvel made a KISS comic, and the red ink was mixed with the band's own blood. It sounds like the fakest thing on this list.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Make Pat call it.]

It's real. February 21st, 1977, backstage at Nassau Coliseum on Long Island, a registered nurse drew a vial of blood from each guy in KISS. Then it went into a fridge, under guard. Then it got flown to the ink plant in Buffalo, and poured into the vats of red ink. A notary signed a paper that starts, "This is to certify that Kiss members Gene Simmons, Ace Frehley, Paul Stanley, and Peter Criss have each donated blood."

The cover said it right on the front: "Printed in real KISS blood."

Stan Lee himself flew up to Buffalo for it. His reaction when he heard the plan: "You guys are out of your mind." He went anyway. Here's Stan's whole business philosophy in one sentence: "Publicity is really one of the most important things in the world." One of the artists remembers somebody setting off a cherry bomb under Stan Lee's seat. On the trip. For the blood.

It cost a buck fifty, when most Marvel comics were thirty cents. In today's money, that's about eight bucks. Marvel printed around 900,000 and sold roughly half a million.

Now, did the blood actually end up in the comics? The story goes there was a mix-up at the printer, and the KISS blood went into a run of Sports Illustrated instead. Nobody's ever proven that. But somewhere out there is a sports fan who might be holding Gene Simmons.

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: and here's the best part, nobody will take credit. Gene Simmons, a man who has taken credit for everything, says "someone came up with the idea of putting real blood in the ink," and that it "wasn't me." Stan Lee says "some publicity guy got the idea." So That Guy is a publicity guy with no name. Wherever you are, sir: you did it.

Verdict: FACT.

## THE STORY IN ONE LINE
Paul McCartney taught Michael Jackson that owning song publishing is where the money is. Michael then bought the Beatles' songs out from under him.

## THE CHAIN (to confirm)
- Early 80s: Michael and Paul are friends, record "The Girl Is Mine" and "Say Say Say." Paul (who owned lots of other people's catalogs, like Buddy Holly's) explains music publishing to Michael. The story goes Michael said something like "I'm going to buy your songs someday." Find the real quote and who heard it.
- 1985: ATV Music (which held most Lennon-McCartney songs) goes up for sale. Paul and Yoko reportedly decided it was too expensive. Michael's lawyer John Branca runs the bid. Michael wins, around $47.5 million.
- After: Paul has to pay to use songs he wrote; Beatles songs end up in commercials (the Nike "Revolution" ad, 1987, and the lawsuit). The friendship dies. The catalog later merges into Sony/ATV.

## THAT GUY (likely)
Paul McCartney, for giving Michael the tip. Suspects: Yoko and Paul for passing on the bid, the lawyer who ran it.

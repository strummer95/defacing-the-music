## THE DAVE MATTHEWS BAND BUS
[CLIP: That's not water, buddy]
A tour bus dumped its toilet tank on a boat full of tourists. And the band offered DNA samples to prove it wasn't them.

The legend: the Dave Matthews Band's tour bus drove over a bridge in Chicago and dropped its entire sewage tank onto an architecture tour boat.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Make Pat call it before you tell him which band.]

August 8th, 2004. A sunny Sunday. The one o'clock architecture boat tour, open top, more than a hundred people on deck, slides under the Kinzie Street bridge. That bridge has an open steel grate for a deck. You can see the sky through it.

Up top, a tour bus rolls across. And about eight hundred pounds of human waste comes through the grate. One report says about two thirds of the people on that boat got soaked.

The tour guide, trying to keep it together, tells everybody it appears some water has hit the deck. And a man in the back, according to Chicago magazine's oral history, says: "That's not water, buddy."

So whose bus? The band's publicist says all their buses were parked. Then the band offers DNA samples. From the band. To compare with the boat. Investigators said no thanks.

What got them was a gym. The East Bank Club, right next to the bridge, had security cameras. The tape caught violin player Boyd Tinsley's bus on the bridge at 1:18 in the afternoon, with one guy on it: the driver, Stefan Wohl. The switch that empties the tank was behind his seat. Prosecutors talked to engineers about it, and the prosecutor said: "We were satisfied with what they told us that it could not have been an accident."

Wohl pleaded guilty. Ten thousand dollar fine. In today's money, that's about seventeen thousand. The band settled with the state for two hundred thousand. In today's money, that's about three hundred and thirty thousand. And for five years, they had to keep a written log of every single time a bus emptied its tank.

Verdict: FACT. All of it. Even the DNA.

And this is a band that played its very first gig at an Earth Day festival. Fifteen years after Chicago, the United Nations made them environmental Goodwill Ambassadors. Somebody in that meeting had to say, "Let's not bring up the boat."

Before we get to the island, you have to meet Billy.
William McFarland. Born December 1991, in New York. And from early on, Billy is a "founder." Not of anything in particular. Just a founder. As a lifestyle.

Magnises is an invitation-only club for young professionals, built around a charge card. You join, you get the card, and the card gets you into exclusive events. The pitch is basically, "You are the kind of person who gets into things."

## THE ISLAND
Okay. The promo video says this is happening on a private island once owned by Pablo Escobar.
[Pause.]
It was not owned by Pablo Escobar.
The island in the video is Norman's Cay. And Norman's Cay does have a drug history. It was tied to Carlos Lehder, a Medellín cartel guy who worked with Escobar. So Billy didn't make up an island with a cocaine past. He just picked the wrong drug lord. Right cartel, wrong employee.
And then they lose the island. The organizers get kicked off Norman's Cay, and the most repeated version is that the Escobar line in the ads didn't help. Whatever the exact reason, the island's gone, and there are about four months left to find somewhere else to throw the greatest party in the world.
So it moves to Great Exuma. Not a private island. A big island with people living on it. And the site, when guests finally see it, gets described as an unfinished gravel lot.

## THE WARNING SIGNS
It's easy to laugh at rich kids who wanted to party with supermodels. And we will. But most of the warning signs were behind the curtain.
In early April, the Wall Street Journal reported that the organizers had missed a series of deadlines to make advance payments to the performers.
[Pause.]
If you've ever played for money, you know what that means. If the promoter can't pay the band, the promoter can't pay anybody.
And Billy's raising money the whole time. Later, in court, he admitted he defrauded eighty investors and faked documents to get more money for this thing. So the money that should be building bathrooms is coming from people being lied to about how much money there is.
Then, the day before it starts, Blink-182 pulls out. Their statement said they weren't confident they'd have what they needed to give fans the kind of show they always give.
That's a polite way of saying, "We looked at it."
Blink-182 is not a fussy band. And they looked at it and went, "Nope."

## THE FALLOUT
Billy McFarland got arrested by federal agents on June 30th, 2017, and charged with wire fraud.
And then, while out on bail, he ran another scheme. Selling fake tickets to exclusive events.
[Pause.]
While on bail. For a fake exclusive event. He sold more fake exclusive events.
In March 2018 he pleaded guilty. That October he was sentenced to six years in federal prison and ordered to forfeit twenty-six million dollars. He got out early, in 2022. As of 2025, reports say he still owed roughly that whole twenty-six million.
Ja Rule got named in a hundred-million-dollar class action. In 2019 a judge dismissed him from it, saying there wasn't enough to show he committed fraud. He was never criminally charged. And in a recorded staff meeting that the New York Times reported on, he summed it up himself: "False advertising, maybe, not fraud."
In January 2019, two documentaries come out four days apart. Hulu drops "Fyre Fraud" with no warning. Then Netflix drops "Fyre." And they go to war. The Hulu side points out that the Netflix film was made with Jerry Media, the company that did Fyre's social media marketing in the first place. The Netflix director says Billy told him Hulu offered Billy two hundred fifty grand for an interview. Hulu's director called that number a lie. Nobody's settled who's right.
So even the movies about the scam had a scam argument.
And Billy announced a Fyre Festival 2. In April 2025 it got postponed indefinitely. Then he sold the Fyre brand on eBay, for about two hundred forty-five thousand dollars.
[Pause.]
The most exclusive brand in the world. Sold on the same website where you buy a used carburetor.

## THE SERIOUS PART
Okay. I need to slow down, because the people who got hurt worst by Fyre weren't the ones with the expensive tickets.
That site was built by Bahamian workers. Locals who hauled, built, cooked and cleaned for weeks. And a lot of them were never paid.
One of them was Maryann Rolle. She ran a restaurant on Exuma and catered for the festival. When the money never came, she used about fifty thousand dollars of her own savings to pay the people who'd worked for her.
[Pause.]
After the Netflix documentary told her story in 2019, a fundraiser for her blew way past its goal, well over a hundred thousand dollars. Ja Rule apologized to her publicly. Good. But she never should have needed any of it.

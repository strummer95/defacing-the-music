## THE WARNING SIGNS
It's easy to laugh at rich kids who wanted to party with supermodels. And we will. But most of the warning signs at Fyre were behind the curtain.
In early April, the Wall Street Journal reported that the organizers had missed a series of deadlines to make advance payments to the performers.
If the promoter can't pay the band, the promoter can't pay anybody.
And Billy's raising money the whole time. Here's how he did it, according to the government. He showed investors documents that inflated Fyre's revenue. And he doctored a stock statement so it looked like shares he owned were worth two and a half million dollars.
They were actually worth less than fifteen hundred.
He took a four-digit number and made it seven digits. That's a craft.
Later, in court, he admitted he defrauded eighty investors. So the money that should be building bathrooms is coming from people being lied to about how much money there is. The original caterer, Starr Events, the company of a famous restaurateur named Stephen Starr, pulled out weeks before the festival. So the food plan is already gone.
The day before it starts, Ja Rule tweets: "The stage is set!!! In less than 24 hours, the first annual Fyre Festival begins. #festivallife."
And that same day, Blink-182 pulls out. Here's their tweet, word for word: "Regrettably, and after much careful and difficult consideration, we want to let you know that we won't be performing at Fyre Fest in the Bahamas this weekend and next weekend. We're not confident that we would have what we need to give you the quality of performances we always give fans."
That's a polite way of saying, "We looked at it." Blink-182 is not a fussy band. And they looked at it and went, "Nope."

## THE SECOND SCAM
[CLIP: Selling Met Gala tickets on bail]
While he was out on bail for faking an exclusive festival, Billy McFarland started selling tickets to the Met Gala. He didn't have any.
Billy got arrested by federal agents on June 30th, 2017, and charged with wire fraud. He gets out on bail. And while he's waiting for his trial, he starts a company called NYC VIP Access. It sells tickets to the most exclusive stuff on Earth. The Met Gala. Coachella. Burning Man. The Super Bowl.
None of the tickets existed.
And who's he selling them to? According to federal prosecutors, he went after the people who bought tickets to Fyre Festival, and the members of his old club, Magnises. He hid his name from the company so they wouldn't notice it was him.
[Pause.]
He went back to the same people. The ones who'd just come home from the gravel lot. And went, "Hey. Exclusive event. You in?"
And some of them were. The AP reported he took in more than a hundred thousand dollars from at least fifteen people. ## THE SERIOUS PART
Okay. I need to slow down, because the people who got hurt worst by Fyre Festival weren't the ones with the expensive tickets.
That site was built by Bahamian workers. Locals who hauled, built, cooked and cleaned for weeks. And a lot of them were never paid.
One of them was Maryann Rolle. She ran a restaurant on Exuma and catered for the festival. When the money never came, she used about fifty thousand dollars of her own life savings to pay the people who'd worked for her. In today's money, that's about sixty-five thousand.
After the Netflix documentary told her story in 2019, a fundraiser for her blew way past its goal, well over a hundred thousand dollars. Ja Rule apologized to her publicly. Good. But she never should have needed any of it.

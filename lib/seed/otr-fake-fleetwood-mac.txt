## THE FAKE FLEETWOOD MAC
[CLIP: Fleetwood Mac with no Fleetwood Mac]
In 1974, Fleetwood Mac went on an American tour, and nobody in Fleetwood Mac was in it.

The legend: their manager stole the name and sent a fake band out to play.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Ask Pat how long it would take him to notice.]

1973. Fleetwood Mac is falling apart. The guitar player, Bob Weston, is having an affair with Mick Fleetwood's wife. So Mick cancels the American tour.

Their manager, Clifford Davis, has a different plan. He owns the name, he says. So he puts together a new band. A singer named Elmer Gantry, a guitar player named Kirby Gregory, a bass player, a drummer. The singer says Davis told them Mick Fleetwood himself was on board, and would join them when the tour started.

Mick Fleetwood never shows up.

January 16th, 1974. Pittsburgh. The promoter, Rich Engler, asks Davis when Fleetwood Mac is getting there. Engler says Davis told him, "They're here."

They were not there.

And the shows go on. Engler says that first crowd "was going wild and loving it." A week and a half later, in New York, the fake Fleetwood Mac's opening act is a brand new band called KISS.

Then word gets out. The real band's Bob Welch calls the tour "a rip-off." Shows start getting canceled. And Clifford Davis tells Rolling Stone: "This band is my band. This band has always been my band."

Lawsuits. About two years of them. Mick Fleetwood wrote in his memoir, "To this day, I don't know the names of the musicians involved and I don't wanna know."

Verdict: FACT. And here's the twist. Fighting over their own name pushed the real band to start their own company and move everything to America. Which is how they ended up in California, right around the time they met two kids named Lindsey Buckingham and Stevie Nicks.

The fake Fleetwood Mac accidentally built the real one.

## THE STORY
New Zealand, this month. The Ten Tenors, which is exactly what it sounds like, ten guys singing very loud, have a real show coming up in Auckland. February 28th, 2027, at the Aotea Centre. That one's legit.

But on StubHub, there was a second show. March 1st, the very next night, at a totally different venue across town. And nobody booked it. Not the band. Not the promoters. Not the venue.

The promoters found out because somebody told them StubHub was selling tickets to it. The venue told the New Zealand Herald: "We have not listed this event for sale." The promoters had to put out a statement just to point people back to their own show: "The actual Auckland concert is at the Aotea Centre on February 28, 2027."

So who made the fake one? Nobody asked for it. Not the band, not the promoters, not the venue. Nobody called StubHub by accident. The Herald asked StubHub. StubHub didn't answer. So either StubHub made up a concert, or StubHub let some random person make up a concert, and then sold tickets to it. I honestly don't know which one is worse.

They've taken it down now. The Herald says you could still find it on Google. And New Zealand's Commerce Commission, which got two complaints, says: "We will complete an assessment of these concerns." Which might be the most New Zealand sentence ever written.

The band didn't book it. The venue didn't book it. The promoters didn't book it. StubHub just had it. For sale.

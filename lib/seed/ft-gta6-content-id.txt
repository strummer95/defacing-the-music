## THE STORY
GTA 6 comes out. Everybody on YouTube streams it. Ten-hour streams. Huge.

One of the artists on the soundtrack, a Colombian reggaeton singer called Frank El Santo, also streams it. And then, apparently, his gameplay videos get registered in YouTube's copyright robot, Content ID. That's the thing that scans every upload and flags anything that matches protected stuff. Normally that's songs and movies. Not his song, though. His gameplay. Frank, playing GTA 6.

And since everybody's playing the same game, everybody's video looks like his. So the robot thinks anybody playing GTA 6 is stealing from Frank, and it files the claims in his name. On other people's videos.

Big channels get flagged. Even streams with the music turned off. One streamer said, "Ppl are losing 10 hour streams to him."

DarkViperAU, one of the biggest GTA channels, messaged him: "Yo my dude, you have put your GTA 6 playthrough in the Content ID system, and so are claiming GTA 6 gameplay livestreams. Please fix."

Frank's apology: "I'm really sorry. I'm not filing these claims manually, and I don't own any rights to GTA 6."

YouTube says it fixed it. But for one weekend, the biggest video game ever made apparently belonged to one reggaeton guy.

## THE STORY
GTA 6 comes out. Everybody on YouTube streams it. Ten-hour streams. Huge.

One of the artists on the GTA 6 soundtrack, a Colombian reggaeton singer called Frank El Santo, plays it and streams it like everybody else. And then, apparently, YouTube decides his videos are the original. The real GTA 6. And everybody else playing it is copying Frank.

YouTube has a robot called Content ID. It scans every upload and flags anything that matches something protected. So now every GTA 6 video looks like a copy of Frank's. Same game, same screens. And the robot starts flagging everybody, in his name. Even streams with the music turned off.

One streamer said, "Ppl are losing 10 hour streams to him."

DarkViperAU, one of the biggest GTA channels, messaged him: "Yo my dude, you have put your GTA 6 playthrough in the Content ID system, and so are claiming GTA 6 gameplay livestreams. Please fix."

Frank's apology: "I'm really sorry. I'm not filing these claims manually, and I don't own any rights to GTA 6."

YouTube says it fixed it. But for one weekend, the biggest video game ever made apparently belonged to one reggaeton guy.

## WHAT THIS IS
Here's the deal.
Every episode, I take one real night from music history where everything went sideways. Fires. Riots. Arrests. Explosions. A guy biting something he really, really should not have bitten.
And I tell you the whole thing. Not the Wikipedia version. Not "this happened, then this happened." I want to tell it to you the way somebody tells you a story at a bar. Where they stop halfway through and go, "Wait. Wait. You've never heard about this?"
[Pause.]
Think of it like the one teacher you had who'd blow off the lesson plan to tell you something insane that actually happened. And you learned more in those ten minutes than in the whole semester.
That's the energy. Except this classroom has a two drink minimum.

## THE NAME
The name. Defacing the Music.
You know the phrase "facing the music." It means you finally have to deal with the consequences of what you did.
And here's your first fun fact of the show: nobody's totally sure where that phrase comes from. There are a couple of theories. One of them is about nervous performers on stage, having to turn and face the orchestra pit, whether they're ready or not.
I like that one. Because it means the whole phrase was born from somebody standing in front of a crowd, about to bomb.
[RIFF: if you know another theory you like, toss it in. If not, use this:]
There are other theories. They're all probably wrong. Pick your favorite. That's how history works most of the time anyway.
So. Defacing the Music. These are the people who were supposed to face the music, and instead they drew a mustache on it, and set it on fire.

## HOUSE RULES
A couple house rules, and then we're done with the syllabus.
Rule one. Every episode is about fifteen minutes. One story. One beer. Out the door. It's a commute. It's a dog walk. It's a long shower if you're going through something.
Rule two. It's all real.
That's the whole point of this show. These stories sound made up. They're not. But the internet is full of fake music facts that have been repeated so many times they feel true. So if a detail is shaky, if it's one of those things everybody says and nobody can prove, I'm going to tell you.
You'll hear me say "the story goes," or "the legend goes." That's me saying: I'm passing this along, but I wasn't there, and neither was the guy who told me.
[FILL: "I looked this up twice" is going to be a recurring phrase on this show. Get used to it.]
Rule three. We punch up.
Some of these nights, real people got hurt. Some of them died. When that happens, I'm going to tell you straight, and they are not the joke.
The joke is the promoter charging four bucks for water. The joke is the label guy who signed off on it. The joke is the guy who looked at a terrible idea and said, "Yeah, that's probably fine."
There's always a guy. This show is about finding that guy.

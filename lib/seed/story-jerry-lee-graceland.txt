## FERRIDAY
To understand the gate, you have to understand Jerry Lee. So let's back up. A lot.
Jerry Lee Lewis is born in September 1935 in Ferriday, Louisiana. Small town. Poor family. Deep Assembly of God, which is a church where people get the Holy Spirit and it shows.
And young Jerry Lee is obsessed with the piano. So his father, Elmo, mortgages the family home for three hundred dollars. And buys him a used upright piano.
[Pause.]
Mortgages the house. For a piano. For a kid.

And here's a weird true fact about Ferriday. Jerry Lee grows up with two cousins. One is Mickey Gilley, who becomes a country star. And the other is Jimmy Swaggart, who becomes one of the biggest TV preachers in America.
So out of one family, in one tiny town, you get a rock and roll wild man, a country singer, and a televangelist. And all three of them can play the piano. That's not a family, that's a talent show at a church picnic that got out of hand.

## SUN
In 1956, Jerry Lee goes to Memphis. To Sun Records. Sam Phillips' label. The little studio on Union Avenue where Elvis got started.
And he gets work there as a session piano player.
And on December 4th, 1956, he's at a Carl Perkins session, playing piano, when Elvis Presley walks in. Elvis is already the biggest star in the world by then. He'd left Sun for RCA, but he dropped by to say hi.
And they start jamming. Gospel songs, country songs, whatever. Johnny Cash shows up at some point. And there's a tape running.
The next day the Memphis Press-Scimitar runs a story and calls them the Million Dollar Quartet. Elvis, Johnny Cash, Carl Perkins, and the new kid on piano.
[Pause.]
So that's the first time Jerry Lee and Elvis are in a room together. Friends. Jamming. Singing hymns around a piano at the studio where both of them got their start.
Hold that picture. Twenty years later, one of them's at the gate.

## THE KILLER
And then Jerry Lee blows up. 1957. "Whole Lotta Shakin' Goin' On." "Great Balls of Fire." He plays standing up, kicking the piano bench across the stage, hair flying. They call him The Killer.
And here's my favorite Jerry Lee legend, and this goes in the "legend goes" pile, so hold it loosely.
The story goes that at a big package show, Jerry Lee was angry that Chuck Berry was closing instead of him. So at the end of his set, he poured gasoline on his piano, set it on fire, walked off, and said something like, "Follow that."
[Pause.]
Is it true? It gets told everywhere. I've never seen it nailed down. Nobody can agree on the city, and there's no picture of a burning piano.
But it's such a Jerry Lee move that I have to tell it, because even if it never happened, everybody who knew him believed it could have.
[FILL: If it didn't happen, somebody made up the greatest exit line in rock and roll, and gave it away for free.]

## 1958
And then 1958 happens.
Jerry Lee goes to England on tour. And at the airport, a reporter asks the girl he's traveling with who she is.
And she says she's his wife.
Her name is Myra Gale Brown. She's his cousin. First cousin once removed. And she's thirteen years old.
[Pause.]
The tour is canceled after three shows. In America, the radio drops him. His price goes from big money to bar money, more or less overnight. Rock and roll's next big thing turns into the guy nobody will book.
I'm not making a joke here. There's no joke in it.
He spends the next decade clawing back, and eventually he does it through country music in the late sixties. And by the seventies he's a big country star again. Still The Killer. Still drinking. Still carrying guns. And that's where we pick him up.

## 1976
1976 is a very bad year for Jerry Lee Lewis.
In September, the day before his forty first birthday, he's at home in Collierville, outside Memphis, with a .357 Magnum. And he points it at a Coke bottle, and he fires.
And he shoots his bass player. Butch Owens. In the chest.
[Pause.]
Owens survived. Jerry Lee said it was an accident, he told police he didn't know the gun was loaded. Owens sued him. And here's the thing to hold onto. The man shot his own bass player in September. And two months later nobody has taken the guns away.

And now we're at November. And Elvis.

## THE GATE
A few hours later. Three in the morning-ish. November 23rd.
Jerry Lee comes back to Graceland. Different car. A Lincoln Continental. One account says the Rolls got traded in for it, which, given the Rolls is upside down, makes sense.
He pulls up to the music gates. And the guard on duty is family. Most accounts say it's Elvis's cousin, Harold Loyd. And the guard has already been told: don't let him in.
Now here's where the stories split, so I'll give you all of them.
The guard's version: Jerry Lee is hollering and screaming and waving a derringer in the air, and demanding to see Elvis.
Other reports say the gun was just on the dashboard. One account says he bumped the gate with the car. That one I can't nail down.
What's pretty solid: the guard calls the police.
And the officer who shows up, Billy J. Kirkpatrick, said later that the gun was a .38 derringer, and it was loaded. And cocked.
[Pause.]
And then there's the champagne bottle. Jerry Lee has been drinking champagne. And at some point he goes to throw the empty bottle out of the car. Except the window isn't open.
And the bottle goes through the window. And Jerry Lee cuts his face on the glass.
[FILL: I want everybody to appreciate that the most violent thing that happened at the gates of Graceland that night was Jerry Lee Lewis versus his own car window. And the window won.]
And Jerry Lee said later, about the champagne: he was drinking a lot of it, he didn't even like champagne, and it made him a little wild.

A little.

## JERRY LEE'S SIDE
Now let me give you Jerry Lee's version, because he gave it for the rest of his life.
His story is that Elvis called him. That he was at a club in Memphis, and Elvis called and told him to come over and hang out. So he did.
And the gun? He said it was a gift from a club owner, he'd forgotten it was even in the car, and it slid off the dash. He never waved it at anybody. And he never, ever, meant to shoot Elvis.
And nobody from Elvis's camp ever confirmed or denied that call. So that's a "story goes." It might be true. It might be champagne.

## ELVIS'S SIDE
And Elvis? This is the part everybody wants to know.
Elvis was home. He was upstairs. And he did not come down.
What exactly he did, that's another split. Elvis's uncle Vester, who worked the gate, said Elvis told the staff to ignore it. Another version says Elvis told them to call the cops.
And the legend goes that Elvis watched the whole thing happen on a closed-circuit security monitor in the house. That's been reported. I've never seen it confirmed. So, hold it loosely.
[Pause.]
But years later, Jerry Lee told the writer Rick Bragg, for his 2014 book, that he thought Elvis told the cops to lock him up. And he said, to be scared of him, knowing him the way Elvis did, was ridiculous.
He also said he never saw Elvis again after that.
[Pause.]
And he didn't. Because nine months later, on August 16th, 1977, Elvis died. At Graceland. He was forty two.
So the last time those two guys were anywhere near each other, one was upstairs, and the other one was in the back of a police car with a cut on his face. Twenty years after they were singing hymns around a piano at Sun.

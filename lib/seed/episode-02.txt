## COLD OPEN
On the last night of Woodstock '99, somebody handed out candles.
Thousands of them. It was supposed to be a peace vigil, for the kids who died at Columbine three months earlier. A beautiful idea.
They handed open flames to a crowd that had been baking on concrete for three days with four dollar water.
A few hours later, the place was on fire. And on stage, the Red Hot Chili Peppers were playing a Jimi Hendrix song called "Fire."
I could not write that. Nobody could write that.

## THE SETUP
July 1999. Thirty years after the original Woodstock, the promoters decide to do it again. Peace, love, music.
Except instead of a farm, they pick Griffiss Air Force Base in Rome, New York. A decommissioned military base. Which means the main thing on the ground is concrete. And tarmac. Miles of it.
Four days, Thursday to Sunday. Around two hundred thousand people. It's late July, and it's in the high nineties, pushing a hundred. No trees. No shade. Just tarmac radiating heat back into your face.
And you can't bring in your own food or water. So now you have to buy it from them.
A bottle of water: four bucks. In 1999 money. Do that math on a hundred degree day.

## CHEESE CARDBOARD
Then there's the food.
A slice of pizza was twelve dollars. OK. I'm from Chicagoland. I have definitely had pizza that was worth twelve bucks a slice. Even in 1999 money.
But Woodstock wasn't selling twelve dollar pizza. They were selling cheese cardboard. And charging twelve dollars for it.
[RIFF: Chicago pizza rant. Go as long as you want.]
So needless to say, a diet of Woodstock "pizza" and warm beer is not something the human body enjoys digesting. Even if you're nineteen and invincible.
Consequently, by Saturday, the plumbing gives out. The porta-potties overflow. Water lines break. So now you don't just have an exhausted crowd. You've got two hundred thousand people standing ankle deep in something that is not mud, staring at each other like extras in Mad Max. Or like Burning Man attendees, if Burning Man had good music.

## SATURDAY
Saturday night, Limp Bizkit takes the east stage. And here's where the legend kicks in.
The way it usually gets told, the organizers went to Fred and said, "Fred, it's getting crazy out there. They're hot, they're tired, they're hungry, and the ditch weed they're passing around ain't calming anybody down. We need you to go out there and settle everyone down."
Now, has anybody ever proven that conversation happened? No. That's "the legend goes." But if it did happen, that's like dropping me into a convention of people selling crystals and homeopathic water vials and not expecting me to be an asshole.
[RIFF: crystals convention. This is your line, run with it.]
Here's what we DO know, because there's tape. Earlier that same day, Alanis Morissette played. Nice, mellow set. And Fred gets up there and says, more or less, "Don't let anybody get hurt. But I don't think you should mellow out. That's what Alanis Morissette had you motherfuckers do."
And then they play "Break Stuff."
[RIFF: "Break Stuff" as a choice. As a guy who plays it live: what that song does to a room.]
People rip plywood off the security walls and crowd surf on it. Fred surfs on a piece of plywood. The crowd tears into the sound towers.
To be fair to Fred, and I said I'd try: he did also tell them not to let anybody get hurt. And he's said since that he was getting the crowd to jump, not to burn the place down. But you don't hand a crowd like that a song called "Break Stuff" and act surprised when they take it literally.

## SUNDAY
Sunday is the last night. And that's the candles.
An anti-gun-violence group hands out candles for a vigil during the Chili Peppers' set. Lovely idea. Except by now, the walls are plywood, the ground is covered in trash, and the crowd has been cooking for three days.
So the candles start fires. People feed the fires with the walls. And the Red Hot Chili Peppers, who had been asked to pay tribute to Jimi Hendrix, play Hendrix's "Fire."
[RIFF: Anthony Kiedis looking out at actual fires while singing "Fire." Pause here.]
After the music stops, it turns into a riot. Vendor booths get torn apart and looted. Trucks get set on fire. ATMs get broken into. State troopers come in to clear the field.

## THE SERIOUS PART
Now I'm going to stop joking for a second.
Three people died at Woodstock '99. A twenty four year old named David DeRosia collapsed during Metallica's set, from the heat. A woman was hit by a car. A forty four year old man died with a heart condition. And women reported being sexually assaulted in the crowds.
Those people aren't the joke. They never are on this show.
The joke is the people who looked at a concrete runway in July, charged four bucks for water, and thought that was a plan.

## WHO LET THIS HAPPEN
So who let it happen?
Fred Durst is the easy answer, and he's been the easy answer for twenty five years. Netflix made a documentary, and he's the face of it.
But Limp Bizkit didn't pick a concrete Air Force base. Limp Bizkit didn't price the water. Limp Bizkit didn't hand out candles to a crowd standing on a pile of firewood.
[RIFF: your verdict. Fair? Unfair? As the Counterfeit guy, defend him or don't.]
There's always a guy. This time, it was a whole office full of guys.

## IDEA (main episode, not a script yet)
Duck asked for this one (Nov 25): "Napster fallout." Everybody knows "Metallica sued Napster"; almost nobody knows how weird the fallout got. Research before writing; everything below is from memory and must be confirmed.

## THE ANGLE
Not "piracy is bad" or "the labels were evil." The chain of decisions: a college kid's dorm-room program, a band that found its own unreleased song already out there, and a fight that turned a heavy metal drummer into the face of the music business. The ending should feel inevitable: of course they hauled boxes of usernames to an office.

## BEATS TO CONFIRM
- Shawn Fanning, about 19, builds Napster in 1999. How fast it grew (millions of users within a year), colleges banning it for clogging their networks.
- Metallica finds "I Disappear" (the Mission: Impossible II song) on Napster before it's released, and radio stations playing it. They sue in April 2000.
- Lars Ulrich shows up at Napster's office in May 2000 with boxes of paper listing hundreds of thousands of usernames caught sharing Metallica songs (the number usually quoted is about 335,000). Find who drove, what the boxes looked like, what Napster said, the press scene.
- Dr. Dre sues too, with his own list.
- The backlash: fans booing, "Napster Bad," the Flash cartoon that made Lars a cartoon villain and Metallica a punchline; the MTV VMAs bit with Lars (2000).
- The irony beat: Metallica's own early days spread through fan tape trading.
- Napster shuts down in 2001. Then the labels start suing regular people, including a 12-year-old in New York (2003) whose mom settled.
- Where Are They Now: Fanning, Sean Parker (Facebook), Lars and Fanning appearing together years later, the band's later take.

## THAT GUY CANDIDATES
Lars (the boxes), the labels' lawyers (suing a 12-year-old), or Napster's own executives (who knew what they had). Verdict depends on what the research shows.

## CAREFUL
- No legal or political takes on copyright; tell what people did and said.
- The kid sued in 2003 is a private person: no name on air unless it was widely public and her family spoke publicly; keep her out of the joke.

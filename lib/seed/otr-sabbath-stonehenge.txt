## BLACK SABBATH'S STONEHENGE
[CLIP: Stonehenge, three times too big]
Black Sabbath once built a Stonehenge so big it wouldn't fit in any arena on Earth.

The legend: this is where Spinal Tap got its Stonehenge joke.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Make Pat call it before you tell him which way the joke went.]

1983. Ozzy's long gone, and Black Sabbath's new singer is Ian Gillan, the guy from Deep Purple. New album, "Born Again." And their manager, Don Arden, has an idea. Geezer Butler: "He came up with the idea of having the stage set be Stonehenge."

So somebody draws it up. And here's Geezer's version of what went wrong: "He wrote it down in meters but he meant to write it down in feet."

A meter is about three feet. So everything comes out about three times too big. The tallest stone is around forty five to fifty feet tall.

Tony Iommi: "We couldn't believe the size of it when we saw it." Geezer says once the stones were up, "they were almost touching the ceiling."

And then: "There was not a building on Earth that you could fit it into." So most of it never got used. Geezer: "We just had to leave it in the storage area."

And it gets better. The manager also hires a little person to dress as the demon baby from the album cover. Every night he crawls across the top of the tallest stone and falls off while a recorded scream plays. Gillan called it "absolutely horrendous," and he says the mattresses that were supposed to catch the guy had been taken away. So the fall was real.

Then March 1984, "This Is Spinal Tap" comes out. And Spinal Tap's big Stonehenge moment is the opposite: they order one, and it shows up eighteen inches tall. Sabbath reportedly thought the movie stole their story.

Verdict: LEGEND. Spinal Tap shot that scene before Sabbath's tour ever started. Spinal Tap didn't copy Sabbath. Real life just copied Spinal Tap, three times bigger.

Somewhere there's a storage unit with a fifty foot rock in it, and nobody's ever opened it to check.

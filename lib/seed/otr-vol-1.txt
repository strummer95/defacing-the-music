## VAN HALEN'S BROWN M&M'S
[CLIP: Brown M&M's]
Van Halen banned brown M&M's. And it was the smartest thing they ever did.

You've heard this one. It's the go-to joke for spoiled rock stars. Van Halen's contract said there could be no brown M&M's backstage. Not no M&M's. Just the brown ones.

And it's real. Their 1982 tour rider has a section called "Munchies," and right there in the list: "M & M's (WARNING: ABSOLUTELY NO BROWN ONES)." Same rider wants herring in sour cream and Blue Nun wine "to be served at room temperature." Classy operation.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Make Pat call it, and ask him what kind of guy bans one color of candy.]

Here's the part nobody tells you. Van Halen was hauling a giant stage into towns that weren't ready for it: nine eighteen-wheelers, when most bands showed up with three. The contract was huge, and the candy line was buried in the middle of it on purpose. David Lee Roth, in his book Crazy from the Heat:

"So, when I would walk backstage, if I saw a brown M&M in that bowl... well, line-check the entire production. Guaranteed you're going to arrive at a technical error. They didn't read the contract."

It was a test. If they skipped the candy, maybe they skipped the part about how much weight the floor can hold. A smoke detector made of chocolate.

And it caught one. March 30th, 1980. A college gym in Pueblo, Colorado. Tickets were eight fifty. In today's money, that's about thirty-three bucks. Roth says the stage was too heavy and tore up the brand new floor. Then he found brown M&M's backstage:

"I went into full Shakespearean 'What is this before me?' ... and promptly trashed the dressing room. Dumped the buffet, kicked a hole in the door, twelve thousand dollars' worth of fun."

Twelve thousand dollars. In today's money, that's about forty-seven thousand. The school's vice president told the paper the band "ate the lasagna with their hands, threw the food around the room, smeared the food on the walls and on each other."

The newspapers ran the version you know: rock stars wreck a college over candy. And Roth? "Well, who am I to get in the way of a good rumor?"

Verdict: FACT. The ban was real, and it was a safety check. The tantrum was also real. Both things.

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: whoever ran that gym and skipped the candy line. Runner-up: Roth, who saw one brown M&M and decided a door had to die.

## KISS PRINTED THEIR BLOOD IN A COMIC BOOK
[CLIP: KISS blood comic]
KISS put their actual blood in a comic book. With a notary watching.

The legend: in 1977, Marvel made a KISS comic, and the red ink was mixed with the band's own blood. It sounds like the fakest thing on this list.

[TALK: Legend or fact? Make Pat call it.]

It's real. February 21st, 1977, backstage at Nassau Coliseum on Long Island, a registered nurse drew a vial of blood from each guy in KISS. Then it went into a fridge, under guard. Then it got flown to the ink plant in Buffalo, and poured into the vats of red ink. A notary signed a paper that starts, "This is to certify that Kiss members Gene Simmons, Ace Frehley, Paul Stanley, and Peter Criss have each donated blood."

The cover said it right on the front: "Printed in real KISS blood."

Stan Lee himself flew up to Buffalo for it. His reaction when he heard the plan: "You guys are out of your mind." He went anyway. Here's Stan's whole business philosophy in one sentence: "Publicity is really one of the most important things in the world." One of the artists remembers somebody setting off a cherry bomb under Stan Lee's seat. On the trip. For the blood.

It cost a buck fifty, when most Marvel comics were thirty cents. In today's money, that's about eight bucks. Marvel printed around 900,000 and sold roughly half a million.

Now, did the blood actually end up in the comics? The story goes there was a mix-up at the printer, and the KISS blood went into a run of Sports Illustrated instead. Nobody's ever proven that. But somewhere out there is a sports fan who might be holding Gene Simmons.

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: and here's the best part, nobody will take credit. Gene Simmons, a man who has taken credit for everything, says "someone came up with the idea of putting real blood in the ink," and that it "wasn't me." Stan Lee says "some publicity guy got the idea." So That Guy is a publicity guy with no name. Wherever you are, sir: you did it.

Verdict: FACT.

## GENE SIMMONS'S COW TONGUE
[CLIP: Gene's cow tongue]
Gene Simmons had a cow's tongue sewn onto his own. Fact or legend?

The legend: Gene's tongue is so long that it can't be his. In the seventies, kids on school buses argued about it. One columnist in Ottawa remembered the boys on her bus debating "whether Gene Simmons really had a cow tongue grafted to his own tongue."

[TALK: Legend or fact? Ask Pat how he thinks that surgery would even go.]

Let's do the math. A beef tongue weighs about three pounds. A human tongue weighs about two and a half ounces. So for this to be true, Gene would need a bigger head. Snopes pointed out that anybody who's seen a cow tongue at a deli counter knows how huge they are.

It's just his tongue. His explanation, from his memoir: "It really was longer than everyone else's." Thank you, Gene.

The rest of the act was real, though. The blood-spitting? He got it from Dracula movies. Gene: "I thought, that's cool, I should do that onstage." The fire-breathing? He first set his own hair on fire on New Year's Eve 1973 in New York, opening for Blue Öyster Cult, and a roadie had to put him out. How many times has he set his hair on fire since? His answer: "probably six or seven times."

Other tongue legends: it's insured for a million dollars. Never confirmed. It's seven inches long. Never measured. For the record, the Guinness world record for longest tongue is just under four inches, and it doesn't belong to Gene.

And in 2017, a calf was born in Kerrville, Texas, with black markings on its face that looked exactly like Gene's KISS makeup. They named her Genie. So, if anything, the cow got something from Gene.

Verdict: LEGEND.

[THAT GUY STING] That Guy: the first kid on the school bus who said "it's a cow tongue," and every kid who believed him.

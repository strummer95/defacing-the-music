## THE SLAUGHTERHOUSE KID
Let's start at the beginning, because Ozzy's whole life is a setup for this.
John Michael Osbourne. Born December 1948 in Aston, a working class part of Birmingham, England. Factories, smoke, no money. Leaves school around fifteen and starts bouncing through the worst jobs you can think of.
One of them is a slaughterhouse in Digbeth, in Birmingham. And Ozzy's own version of it is incredible. His first job there was cleaning out the stomachs of dead sheep. He says for the first two or three weeks, he did nothing but throw up. Then, quote, "once I'd proved myself as puke remover, they promoted me to cow killer."
That's a career path. Most people's first promotion is "assistant manager." Ozzy's was "cow killer." And you want to know the craziest part? It's going to matter. Later in this story, a guy who used to gut animals for a living is going to have a tour where he throws animal guts at people. I don't know if anybody connected those dots in 1982. I'm connecting them now.
[RIFF: worst job you ever had, and whether it beats puke remover. Or use this:]
I've had bad jobs. I've never had a job where throwing up was the probation period.
He also did a short stretch in Winson Green prison in Birmingham, for a burglary, because he couldn't pay the fine. And when he gets out, he wants to sing. So he puts up a card in a local music shop. Depending on who tells it, it says "Ozzy Zig Needs Gig" or "Ozzy Zig Requires Gig." And at least one version adds the line that sold it: "Has own PA."
That's how you start Black Sabbath. Not talent. Not a demo. The guy owns speakers.
Tony Iommi, Geezer Butler and Bill Ward answer that card, and by 1968 they're a band. They become Black Sabbath, and they basically invent heavy metal. The sound, the darkness, the whole thing. Every band with a black T-shirt owes them money.
And in April 1979, Black Sabbath fires him.
For partying too hard. In the seventies. Think about how much partying you have to do to get fired from Black Sabbath. That's getting kicked out of a frat for being too loud.
[FILL: Sabbath kept going with Ronnie James Dio, who was great. Ozzy, by his own account, holed up in a hotel and mostly just drank.]
And then the person who saves him is Sharon Arden. Her dad, Don Arden, managed Sabbath. Sharon decides Ozzy can be a solo star. She becomes his manager, later his wife, and she builds a band around him, with a young guitar player from California named Randy Rhoads. Keep him in mind. We'll get back to Randy.

## THE DOVE
[CLIP: The doves at the sales convention]
March 27th, 1981. Los Angeles. Ozzy's first solo record, "Blizzard of Ozz," is coming out in America that same day. It's got "Crazy Train" on it. It's going to be huge. Nobody knows that yet.
His American label is part of CBS, and CBS is having a sales convention. A room full of record company people. These are the people who decide how hard they push your record. And Ozzy's relationship with them is, let's say, rocky. He's the drunk guy who got fired from Sabbath.
So the idea, and most tellings put this on Sharon, is to go in and make nice. Bring some white doves. Release them. Peace. Goodwill. "I'm a changed man."
Stop and think about who signed off on this. You have a client whose last job ended because he couldn't stop drinking. And your plan to win over the suits is: put live birds in his jacket and send him into a room full of strangers.
According to the biographer Mick Wall, Ozzy killed a bottle of brandy in the car on the way there.
So he gets in the room. And he lets one dove go. It flies around. Lovely.
And there's a woman from the PR side talking at him, and she's getting on his nerves. And Ozzy pulls out the next dove. And bites its head off.
His own explanation, later, to Wall: he did it just to shut her up.
In one of his tellings, he spits the head out on the table and she ends up on the floor screaming.
[Pause.]
And then he does another one. That's when they threw him out.
And think about the doves for a second. This was Ozzy's side's idea, so somebody in Ozzy's camp had to actually go get them. Live white doves. That was a task on somebody's to-do list. "Pick up dry cleaning. Doves." And whoever that was did their job perfectly. The doves showed up. The doves were in the jacket. And the plan still ended with a bird missing its head and Ozzy getting walked out of the building.
Now, the story changes depending on when you ask Ozzy. Here's the version he gave Sounds magazine, the British music paper. The bird was already dead. They'd planned to release it, it died beforehand, "so rather than waste it, I bit its head off." Rather than waste it. Like it's leftover pizza. And then the line he loved: "You should have seen their faces." He also said, flat out, "It was not a publicity stunt. I was just out of my fucking face."
And there were photos. Rudy Sarzo, who'd just joined the band as bass player, has described a little series of shots of the PR woman's face as it happens. So this wasn't a rumor. It was documented.
Rudy also has the sequel. He says he met Ozzy that same day, at the Ardens' place, and Ozzy had forgotten there was still another dove in his jacket. He found it. You can guess.
[FILL: And nobody can even agree how many doves there were. Two, or three, depending on who's telling it, plus the one he forgot was in his jacket. But every version ends the same way. Ozzy, a room full of record company people, and a dove with no head.]
So did they drop him? Here's what actually happened. They threw him out. Sharon has said they were escorted out and told not to come back. And the record came out anyway, on that same label, and sold millions. They banned the man from the building and kept selling his album. That's the music business in one sentence.

## WHY NOBODY REMEMBERS THE DOVE
Now, everybody knows about the bat. Almost nobody knows about the dove. And the dove is worse. The dove, he did on purpose.
So why isn't it famous?
First, it was a closed room. A sales convention. A room full of music business people, not thousands of fans. It made the music papers, it had photos, but no regular person was there to go home and tell their whole high school.
Second, CBS was not out there bragging about it. "Come see our new artist, he decapitates birds at meetings" is not a press release.
Third, no sequel. Nobody went to the hospital. No rabies. No doctors. No mayor. The bat comes with a whole second act.
And fourth, the big one. A bat fits. Ozzy is the Prince of Darkness. Bats are spooky. Bats are Dracula. A bat is basically merch. A dove is the bird of peace. Biting the head off a dove is just weirdly sad.
So the dove is the one he did on purpose. The bat is the one everybody remembers.

## THE MEAT
So the record's a hit. Then a second one, "Diary of a Madman." And at the end of 1981 Ozzy goes out on the Diary of a Madman tour, with a stage show built to shock. And part of the show is meat.
Raw meat, thrown into the crowd. Pig intestines. Calves' livers. Ozzy had a catapult on stage, and he'd launch piles of raw meat into the audience, along with plastic rats. Every night.
And it gets better. According to a biography of Sharon by Sue Crawford, Sharon sent ads to the towns Ozzy was playing, encouraging fans to bring their own raw meat to throw at him.
[Pause.]
So it's not just "Ozzy throws meat at you." It's "Ozzy throws meat at you, and also, please, bring meat."
Somebody on that tour had to buy the meat. Every day. In a new city. Walk into a butcher shop in Kansas and go, "Yeah, I'll take whatever you're going to throw away. A lot of it. No, don't wrap it." And remember who's at the front of this tour: a guy who used to kill cows for a living. He's back at work. He just found a way to get paid more for it.
And here's what happens when you throw meat at a crowd. The crowd starts throwing stuff back.
Fans start showing up armed. Rubber snakes. Rubber bats. Rubber everything. And then not rubber. People throw sheep testicles on stage. Somebody throws a live frog. It becomes part of the deal. You go to an Ozzy show, you bring something gross, you throw it at Ozzy, and he does something with it.
And word gets around about the dove, too. So now the fans know: this is the guy who bites the heads off things. Bring him something to bite.
So by January 1982, every night, a pile of stuff is landing at his feet. Almost all of it fake.
Almost.

## THE KID
Des Moines, Iowa. Wednesday, January 20th, 1982. Veterans Memorial Auditorium.
And the police in Des Moines have heard about this tour. Before the show, a Des Moines police lieutenant warns Ozzy's manager: if he harms an animal on stage, under Iowa law, he's going to jail. The city knew. The city saw it coming. Just not from the direction it came from.
Because there's a seventeen year old high school kid in the crowd named Mark Neal. And Mark has a bat.
Here's how, and this comes from Mark himself, in the Register. About two weeks before the concert, Mark's younger brother came home from school with a bat. He wanted to keep it. As a pet. And the bat, as bats do when you keep them in a house in Iowa in January, did not make it.
[Pause.]
[FILL: Now, one later story in the Register says the bat was already dead when his brother found it. So maybe it never had a chance. Either way, the Neal house now has a dead bat in it.]
So now the Neal family has a dead bat. And a concert coming up. And Mark's friend, Carmen Martin-Kelly, has an idea: don't throw it out. Freeze it.
So they do. The bat goes in the freezer. For about two weeks. In a family freezer. Next to the, I don't know, the Tater Tots. The ice cream. Mom's going in for a bag of peas and there's a bat looking at her.
And they don't keep it a secret, either. Carmen said later, "We were telling everybody before the concert that we were bringing a bat."
Every one of us knew a kid like Mark in high school. Most of them never got further than a whoopee cushion. Mark went pro.
On the night, Mark seals the bat in a plastic bag and takes it into the show. Some retellings say he hid it in his coat. I can't confirm the coat. But it's Iowa, in January, so I'd bet on the coat.
I can't bring a bottle of water into a concert in 2026. This kid walked into a show with wildlife. In a plastic bag.

## THE BITE
[CLIP: The bite]
Sometime during the show, Mark throws the bat.
And it lands in front of Rudy Sarzo, the bass player. Rudy says he looked down, saw it, and pointed at it with the headstock of his bass. Kept playing. Mark's own description: "He looked down at it and motioned to Ozzy and, as they say, the rest is history."
So Ozzy walks over. And here's the question I couldn't let go of: why couldn't he tell?
A few reasons. One: he'd had weeks of rubber stuff thrown at him. His brain's been trained. Thing lands, thing is rubber. Two: it had been in a freezer for two weeks. It's not flapping around. It's lying there. Three: Ozzy told the BBC in 2006 that he thought it was one of those Halloween joke bats, because it had some string around its neck. Where the string came from, nobody's ever explained, and I've looked. And four: stage lights, loud, adrenaline, and was he drinking? He doesn't say so in his telling. But this is the guy who did a bottle of brandy on the way to a sales meeting.
So in his memoir, "I Am Ozzy," he says he did what he always did when he got a rubber toy on stage. Chomp.
And then. In his words: "Immediately, though, something felt wrong. Very wrong." His mouth was instantly full of "warm, gloopy liquid, with the worst aftertaste you could ever imagine."
And then, he says, "the head in my mouth twitched."
[Pause.]
So, how much did he bite? Did he notice halfway and stop? Not according to anybody who was there. Mark Neal told the Register that he watched Ozzy pick it up, bite its head off, and throw the bat into the audience. So he went all the way through. The head came off. He didn't nibble and check.
What happened to the head after that? Did he spit it out? Swallow it? Hand it to Rudy? Nobody wrote that part down. I went looking. The head's whereabouts are officially unknown.
There's a moment, maybe half a second, where a man's whole brain goes, "Huh. That's not what rubber tastes like." And the rest of his life changes.
And was it alive? This one can't be settled, so I'll give you both. Mark and Carmen always said it was dead. It had been in a freezer for two weeks. Ozzy, for the rest of his life, said it twitched. My money's on the freezer. But it's Ozzy's mouth, not mine.
And a bat expert from Bat Conservation International told TMZ, after he died, that biting through a bat's neck takes real jaw strength. Muscle, tissue, bone. So whatever else you want to say about him, the man had a strong bite.

## THE HOSPITAL
And here's the thing. He finished the show. Rudy says there was an ambulance waiting for Ozzy when they came off, and Sharon told him, "He bit into a bat."
They take him to Broadlawns Medical Center in Des Moines. Rabies shot. Tetanus shot. And here's how the Des Moines Register wrapped it up. He left town with a four day supply of rabies vaccine, the first of five shots already in him, thirty nine thousand dollars from the concert, and, I'm reading this straight from the paper, "a bat taste in his mouth."
Thirty nine thousand dollars and a bat taste in his mouth. That's the most honest summary of a rock and roll night I've ever read in a newspaper.
The hospital phones blow up. A nurse supervisor there, Pam Culver, got calls from England, from Canada, from all over the US. And people were already saying the whole thing was a publicity stunt. So who steps up to shut that down? Not his manager. Not a publicist. The nurse. Pam told the Register, "If it was a joke or publicity stunt, he sure put himself to a lot of trouble to do it." Which is a very polite Iowa way of saying nobody signs up for five rabies shots for the press.
Picture the Prince of Darkness in a waiting room, filling out a clipboard. "Reason for visit." And he has to write it down. In pen.
Then the town loses its mind. Mayor Pete Crivaro wants to know whether Ozzy broke an agreement about not using animals in the act. The auditorium makes a new rule: no performer can use, present, or "in any way" make live animals part of a show without management's consent. Which, again, he didn't. The animal was brought by the audience.
Two nights later he plays Milwaukee, and the local Humane Society sends an officer, William Forss, with the power to arrest Ozzy if any animal gets hurt. Forss told the press they'd heard Ozzy planned to "blow up a goat" on stage. Nobody blew up a goat. The show went fine.
And Mark? Mark didn't hide. He was in the Des Moines Register two days after the show, by name. He said, "It really freaked me out." And then he asked the reporter, "I won't get in any trouble for admitting this, will I?"
[FILL: Forty years later, Rolling Stone ran an anniversary piece that said the bat thrower had never come forward. He came forward that week. In the local paper. With a quote.]
And nobody has ever stepped up to say "no, actually, it was me." If it was somebody else, trust me, they'd have said so. This is the most famous throw in rock history. It was Mark Neal.

## THE ALAMO
[CLIP: Ozzy and the Alamo]
And Ozzy is not done. It's barely a month later.
February 19th, 1982. The tour's in San Antonio, Texas. And Ozzy gets arrested by the Alamo.
Now, the way everybody tells it is "Ozzy peed on the Alamo." The actual spot was the Alamo Cenotaph, which is a sixty foot monument right there on the plaza, built in 1939 to honor the men who died at the Alamo. And the actual charge was public intoxication. The peeing is the story. The drunk is the charge.
The story goes, and this comes from Ozzy's own retellings, that he was wearing one of Sharon's dresses, because she'd hidden his clothes to keep him from going out drinking. Sharon was his girlfriend then, they married that summer. And he went out drinking anyway. In the dress.
That part I can't prove. But it is the most Ozzy detail that has ever existed, and I'm not cutting it.
The concert promoter, a guy named Jack Orbin, posted a forty dollar bond to get him out.
Of all the walls in Texas. And there are a lot of walls in Texas. It's a big state. He found the one monument where people will never, ever forget it.
San Antonio banned him from playing the city for about ten years. In 1992 he gave ten thousand dollars to the Daughters of the Republic of Texas, who took care of the Alamo, and that ended it. And in 2015 he went back with his son Jack, for a TV show, and apologized in person. Thirty three years later. Texas is more forgiving than you'd think. They did not forget, but they forgave.

[CLIP: That Guy verdict]

## THE LEGEND
Now, before we go. Ozzy died on July 22nd, 2025. He was seventy six.
Seventeen days before that, on July 5th, he played one last show, "Back to the Beginning," at Villa Park in Birmingham, his hometown. The original Black Sabbath, back together one more time. He couldn't stand for long anymore, so he sang sitting on a throne. Over forty thousand people in the stadium. The livestream peaked at about five point eight million people watching. The family says it raised around ten or eleven million dollars for charity, after they paid everybody's way in. Not the hundred and ninety million the internet said. Sharon called that number "just ridiculous."
So let's do the numbers.
Over a hundred million records sold, between Sabbath and solo.
"Blizzard of Ozz," the record he was promoting the day of the dove, is certified five times platinum in the US.
He's in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice. Once with Black Sabbath in 2006, once on his own in 2024.
His last solo record, "Patient Number 9," won the Grammy for Best Rock Album. He was in his seventies.
"Crazy Train" got played live over twelve hundred times, going by the fan logged setlists on setlist.fm. And after he died, it hit the Billboard Hot 100 for the first time ever. Forty five years after it came out.
On Spotify, he picked up more than six million monthly listeners in the days after he died. And in August 2026, "Crazy Train" passed one billion streams. His first solo song to do it. "Paranoid," with Sabbath, got there first.
[RIFF: what Ozzy means to you. Or use this:]
A billion streams. From a guy whose first job review was "good puke remover."
And here's the last thing. Ozzy knew exactly how he'd be remembered. He said when he died, the headline would be, "Ozzy Osbourne the bat biter is dead. Bats can now live in peace." And he figured the bats would hear it and go, "Fuck me, thank God he's dead."
And sure, the bat made the obituaries. But so did Prince of Darkness. Godfather of heavy metal. Hall of Famer, twice. The guy who sold a hundred million records and filled a stadium in his hometown on a throne, weeks before he died.
Not bad for a cow killer from Aston.

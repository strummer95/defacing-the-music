## COLD OPEN
A sixteen year old in Birmingham, England, gets a promotion at the slaughterhouse. From puke remover to cow killer.
Seventeen years later, he walks into a record company sales convention with doves in his jacket.
A year after that, a family in Des Moines, Iowa, has a dead bat in the freezer. And their seventeen year old has a ticket to a concert.
All of these things are connected. None of them end well for the animals.

## THE SLAUGHTERHOUSE KID
Let's start at the beginning, because Ozzy's whole life is a setup for this.
John Michael Osbourne. Born December 1948 in Aston, a working class part of Birmingham, England. Factories, smoke, no money. He leaves school around fifteen and starts working the worst jobs there are.
One of them is a slaughterhouse in Digbeth. His first job there is cleaning out the stomachs of dead sheep. Here's Ozzy himself: "For the first two or three weeks, I did nothing but throw up." And then: "Once I'd proved myself as puke remover, they promoted me to cow killer."
Most people's first promotion is assistant manager. Ozzy's was cow killer. Keep that in your pocket. A guy who gutted animals for a living is going to end up on a tour where he throws animal guts at people.
He also does a short stretch in Winson Green prison for a burglary, because he can't pay the fine. When he gets out, he wants to sing. So he puts a card up in a local music shop. Depending on who tells it, it says "Ozzy Zig Needs Gig" or "Ozzy Zig Requires Gig." And at least one version adds the line that sold it: "Has own PA."
That's how you start Black Sabbath. Not talent. Not a demo. The guy owns speakers.
Tony Iommi, Geezer Butler and Bill Ward answer that card. By 1968 they're a band, and as Black Sabbath they basically invent heavy metal.
And in April 1979, Black Sabbath fires him. For partying too hard. In the seventies. Think about how much partying you have to do to get fired from Black Sabbath.
Iommi, years later, to MusicRadar: "It wasn't just my decision to fire Ozzy, it was a band decision. It was either we break up or we carry on without him."
[FILL: Sabbath kept going with Ronnie James Dio, who was great. Ozzy, by his own account, holed up in a hotel and mostly just drank.]
The person who saves him is Sharon Arden. Her dad, Don Arden, managed Sabbath. Sharon decides Ozzy can be a solo star. She becomes his manager, later his wife, and she builds a band around him with a young guitar player from California named Randy Rhoads. We'll get back to Randy.

## THE DOVE
[CLIP: The doves at the sales convention]
March 27th, 1981. Los Angeles. Ozzy's first solo record, "Blizzard of Ozz," is coming out in America that same day. It's got "Crazy Train" on it. It's going to be huge. Nobody knows that yet.
His American label is part of CBS, and CBS is having a sales convention. A room full of the people who decide how hard to push your record. And to them, Ozzy is the drunk guy who got fired from Sabbath.
Here's Ozzy, on a radio special for the record's anniversary: "So Sharon says to me, 'We've got to make an impression.'"
And the impression, and most tellings put this one on Sharon, is doves. Go in, give a little speech, release some white doves. Peace. Goodwill. I'm a changed man.
Stop and think about who signed off on this. Your client's last job ended because he couldn't stop drinking. And your plan to win over the suits is: put live birds in his jacket and send him into a room full of strangers.
And think about the doves themselves. Somebody in Ozzy's camp had to actually go get them. Live white doves were a task on somebody's to do list. "Pick up dry cleaning. Doves." We don't know who that was. But they're going to be busy this year.
According to the biographer Mick Wall, Ozzy had been hitting the brandy hard on the way there.
So he gets in the room. He lets one dove go. It flies around. Lovely.
And there's a woman from the PR side, and she's talking at him, and talking at him. Here's how Ozzy told it to Wall. She was "going on and on," and, quote, "I pulled out one of these doves and bit its fucking head off. Just to shut her up."
In one of his tellings, he spits the head out on the table and she ends up on the floor screaming. And then he does another one. That's when they throw him out.

Now, the story changes depending on when you ask Ozzy. Here's the version he gave Sounds, the British music paper: "The bird was dead... we were planning to release it there, but it died beforehand. So rather than waste it I bit its head off... You should have seen their faces."
Rather than waste it. Like it's leftover pizza.
And here's Ozzy again, later, to anybody who called it a stunt: "It was not a publicity stunt. I was just out of my fucking face on drugs and alcohol."
And there were photos. Rudy Sarzo, who'd just joined the band on bass, has described a little series of shots of the PR woman's face as it happens. Rudy also has the sequel. He says he met Ozzy that same day, at the Ardens' place, and Ozzy had forgotten there was still another dove in his jacket. He found it. You can guess.
So whoever ran that errand bought at least one dove too many. Or exactly the right number, depending on how you look at it.
Billboard, going off Sharon's own book: the executives had them escorted out and told not to come back. And the record came out anyway, on that same label, and sold millions. They banned the man from the building and kept selling his album. That's the music business in one sentence.
So why does nobody remember the dove, when that's the one he did on purpose? Closed room, no hospital, no sequel. And a bat fits the Prince of Darkness. A bat is basically merch. A dove is just weirdly sad.

## THE MEAT
So the record's a hit. Then a second one, "Diary of a Madman." At the end of 1981, Ozzy goes out on the Diary of a Madman tour with a stage show built to shock. And part of the show is meat.
Raw meat, thrown into the crowd. Pig intestines. Calves' livers. Ozzy had a catapult on stage, and he'd launch piles of raw meat into the audience, along with plastic rats. Every night.
And according to a biography of Sharon by Sue Crawford, Sharon sent ads to the towns Ozzy was playing, encouraging fans to bring their own raw meat to throw at him.
So it's not just Ozzy throws meat at you. It's Ozzy throws meat at you, and also, please, bring meat.
Which brings us back to our friend with the to do list. Doves, done. Now: meat. Every day. In a new city. Walk into a butcher shop in Kansas and go, "Yeah, I'll take whatever you were going to throw away. A lot of it. No, don't wrap it." And remember who's at the front of this tour: a guy who used to kill cows for a living. He's back at work. He just found a way to get paid more for it.
And here's what happens when you throw meat at a crowd. The crowd throws stuff back.
Fans start showing up armed. Rubber snakes. Rubber bats. Rubber everything. And then not rubber. People throw sheep testicles on stage. Here's Rudy Sarzo, the bass player: "One time in Texas, they threw a gigantic frog. I thought somebody threw a baby onstage, it was so big."
Rudy says the crew would see Polaroids of what got past security, and one of them was the freshly cut head of a cow. You go to an Ozzy show, you bring something gross, you throw it at Ozzy, and he does something with it.
And word about the dove has gotten around too. So now the fans know: this is the guy who bites the heads off things. Bring him something to bite.
By January 1982, every night, a pile of stuff is landing at his feet. Almost all of it fake.
Almost.

## THE KID
Des Moines, Iowa. Wednesday, January 20th, 1982. Veterans Memorial Auditorium. About five thousand people.
And the police in Des Moines have heard about this tour. Before the show, a police lieutenant named Derald Leaming goes to Ozzy's manager. Here's Leaming in the Des Moines Register: if an animal got harmed, he and Osbourne "were going to jail because it's against Iowa law." And: "We had heard about Osbourne sticking birds in his mouth and warned them he would be arrested on the spot if he bit them."
So the city saw it coming. They were watching for birds. They were looking the wrong direction.
Because there's a seventeen year old high school kid in the crowd named Mark Neal. And Mark has a bat.
Here's how, and this comes from Mark himself, in the Register. About two weeks before the concert, Mark's younger brother came home from school with a bat. He wanted to keep it. As a pet. And the bat, as bats do when you keep them in a house in Iowa in January, did not make it.
[FILL: Now, a later story in the Register says the bat was already dead when his brother found it. So maybe it never had a chance. Either way, the Neal house has a dead bat in it.]
And Mark's friend, Carmen Martin-Kelly, has an idea: don't throw it out. Freeze it.
So the bat goes in the family freezer. For about two weeks. Next to the Tater Tots. Mom goes in for a bag of peas and there's a bat looking at her.
And where did that idea come from? Carmen had heard the dove story. She figured a bat was the right gift for the Prince of Darkness.
And they don't keep it quiet, either. Here's Carmen, in the Register: "We were telling everybody before the concert that we were bringing a bat. Hell or high water, we were gonna get it in and throw the bat up onstage."
Every one of us knew a kid like Mark in high school. Most of them never got further than a whoopee cushion. Mark went pro.
On the night, Mark seals the bat in a plastic bag and takes it into the show. Some retellings say he hid it in his coat. I can't confirm the coat. But it's Iowa, in January, so I'd bet on the coat.

## THE BITE
[CLIP: The bite]
Sometime during the show, Mark throws the bat. Here's Mark: "It landed in front of Rudy Sarzo, the bass player. He looked down at it and motioned to Ozzy and, as they say, the rest is history."
Rudy's side matches. Here's Rudy, to Metal Hammer: "The bat actually fell in front of me." He says he looked over at Ozzy, pointed at it with the headstock of his bass, and kept headbanging.
So Ozzy walks over. And here's the question I couldn't let go of: why couldn't he tell?
He'd had weeks of rubber stuff thrown at him, so his brain had learned that the thing on the floor is rubber. And this one had been in a freezer for two weeks. It wasn't flapping around. It was lying there.
Here's Ozzy on David Letterman, just two months later: "Somebody threw a bat onstage and I thought it was one of these toy bats, so I picked it up, bite the thing's head off and suddenly everybody is freaking out."
And to the BBC in 2006, he added a detail I can't explain. He thought it was "one of them Halloween joke bats 'cos it had some string around its neck." Where the string came from, nobody has ever said.
Now the memoir, "I Am Ozzy." He says he did what he always did with a rubber toy on stage. Chomp. And then: "Immediately, though, something felt wrong. Very wrong." His mouth was instantly full of "warm, gloopy liquid, with the worst aftertaste you could ever imagine."
And then, he says, "the head in my mouth twitched."
[Pause.]
So how much did he bite? Did he notice halfway and stop? Not according to the guy who threw it. Mark told the Register he watched Ozzy pick it up, bite its head off, and throw the bat into the audience. The head came off. He didn't nibble and check.
What happened to the head after that? Spat out? Swallowed? Nobody wrote that part down. The head's whereabouts are officially unknown.
And was it alive? Mark and Carmen always said it was dead. It had been in a freezer for two weeks. Ozzy, for the rest of his life, said it twitched. My money's on the freezer. But it was Ozzy's mouth, not mine.
After he died, TMZ asked Kathryn Slater from Bat Conservation International about it. Her take: getting through a bat's neck takes real jaw strength. Muscle, tissue, bone. And she said most bats aren't rabid, so he probably didn't even need the shots. He got them anyway. A lot of them.

## THE REGISTER
[CLIP: The Des Moines Register reads it back]
I want to read you how the hometown paper wrote it up. This is the Des Moines Register, Friday, January 22nd, 1982, page three, by a reporter named Nick Lamberto. The lead:
"Rock singer Ozzy Osbourne left Des Moines Thursday with a $39,000 haul from a concert at Veterans Memorial Auditorium, a four-day supply of rabies vaccine and a bat taste in his mouth."
That's the first sentence. A newspaper, in 1982, put "a bat taste in his mouth" in the first sentence.
And it keeps going: "After the show, Osbourne went to Mercy Hospital Medical Center, and was referred to Broadlawns Medical Center because rabies vaccine was available there."
"Osbourne, listed as John M. Osbourne, 33, of Beverly Hills, California, was given a tetanus shot and a rabies shot and took enough rabies vaccine with him to complete the series, according to Winifred Barden, a supervisor at Broadlawns."
"Pam Culver, another Broadlawns supervisor, said Osbourne paid $500 to $600 in cash for the medical care and vaccine."
So let's do the math on that night. Thirty nine thousand dollars, which is about a hundred and thirty thousand today. Minus five or six hundred in cash for the rabies, about two thousand today. And the Prince of Darkness checks into the hospital as John M. Osbourne, thirty three, of Beverly Hills. Filling out a clipboard. "Reason for visit." In pen.
Then the auditorium manager, Dave Palmitier, tells the paper what he saw. Osbourne, quote, "did let some doves loose, which he wasn't supposed to do. We found one dead that apparently had been killed in the audience."
[Pause.]
The doves are back. He brought doves to Des Moines. Which means somewhere on that tour, our errand guy is back on the phone. "Hi, do you have doves? Great. And separately, do you sell livers in bulk?"
And then the mayor, Pete Crivaro. He wants to know whether Osbourne "violated an agreement on the use of animals in his act." And: "We had been assured he wasn't going to, and I want to find out who is lying."
Nobody was lying, Pete. The animal came from the audience. The auditorium made a new rule anyway: no performer could use, present, or "in any way" make live animals part of a show without management's permission.
The hospital phones blow up. Pam Culver gets calls from England, from Canada, from all over the US. And people are already saying the whole thing was a publicity stunt. So the nurse supervisor shuts it down. Here's Pam: "If it was a joke or publicity stunt, he sure put himself to a lot of trouble to do it."
Which is a very polite Iowa way of saying nobody signs up for rabies shots for the press.
And Mark? Mark didn't hide. He was in the same paper, by name. "It really freaked me out." And then he asked the reporter: "I won't get in any trouble for admitting this, will I?"
Forty years later, Rolling Stone ran an anniversary piece that said, and I'm quoting it, "The Des Moines bat thrower has never come forward publicly." American Songwriter caught that one. He came forward that week. In the local paper. With a quote.
And nobody has ever stepped up to say no, actually, it was me. It was Mark Neal.

## THE ROAD
And the shots didn't stop in Iowa. Ozzy, describing it years later: "Every night for the rest of the tour I had to find a doctor and get more rabies shots: One in each arse cheek, one in each thigh, one in each arm. Every one hurt like a bastard."
Two nights after Des Moines he plays Milwaukee, and the local Humane Society sends an officer, William Forss, with the power to arrest Ozzy if any animal gets hurt. Forss told the press they'd heard Ozzy planned to "blow up a goat" on stage. Nobody blew up a goat. The show went fine.
Then Champaign, Illinois. Here's the UPI wire, January 27th, 1982: "Rock singer Ozzy Osbourne, undergoing rabies treatment because he bit off the head of a bat, apparently suffered a reaction to the rabies serum and collapsed during a performance, a doctor said."
A clinic doctor called it serum sickness. Vomiting, joint pain. Maybe laryngitis on top. They treated him and let him go.
So that's the bat's revenge. It didn't give him rabies. The cure for rabies knocked him down in the middle of a show.
And the person who saw the upside right away? Rudy Sarzo put it like this: "Sharon knew immediately that she had an opportunity here."

## THE ALAMO
[CLIP: Ozzy and the Alamo]
And Ozzy is not done. February 19th, 1982. San Antonio, Texas. Ozzy gets arrested by the Alamo.
The way everybody tells it is "Ozzy peed on the Alamo." The actual spot was the Alamo Cenotaph, a sixty foot monument on the plaza, built in 1939 to honor the men who died there. And the actual charge was public intoxication. The peeing is the story. The drunk is the charge.
And he was wearing a dress. Here's how that happened. Sharon has said she hid his clothes to keep him from going out drinking. They weren't married yet. And Ozzy, asked about it years later, said, "I'd wear Sharon's clothes." So he went out anyway. In her dress.
Nobody's produced a photo of the dress at the monument. But both of them tell the hiding the clothes part, and I'm not cutting it.
The concert promoter, a guy named Jack Orbin, posted a forty dollar bond to get him out. About a hundred and thirty five bucks today.
Of all the walls in Texas, he picked the one place that comes with its own slogan about not forgetting it. Ozzy did not remember the Alamo. The Alamo remembered Ozzy.
San Antonio banned him from playing the city for about ten years. In 1992 he gave ten thousand dollars, about twenty three thousand today, to the Daughters of the Republic of Texas, who looked after the Alamo, and that ended it. And in 2015 he went back with his son Jack, for a History Channel show, and apologized in person. Thirty three years later.

## THE SERIOUS PART
Now I have to slow down.
On March 19th, 1982, a month after San Antonio, the tour is in Florida. The tour bus driver, who was also a pilot, takes a small plane up from the property where they're parked. Randy Rhoads goes up with him, along with the band's makeup artist, Rachel Youngblood. The plane crashes. All three are killed. Randy was twenty five years old.
Randy was the reason those records sounded the way they did. "Crazy Train." "Mr. Crowley." If you play guitar, you know. He took classical lessons on the road. He was just getting started.

## AFTER THE BAT
And somehow, he keeps going. In July 1982, he marries Sharon. Same year as the bat, the Alamo, and Randy. That's a lot of year.
Here's Ozzy to the Register in 2001: "The name of the town of Des Moines is embossed in my head!" And: "I've had some mileage from Des Moines."
In 1996 the Osbournes start Ozzfest, which runs for years as one of the biggest metal festivals in America.
And in 2002, MTV puts cameras in their house. "The Osbournes." The bat guy, the guy who got arrested by the Alamo, becomes America's confused TV dad, shuffling around his own house yelling "Sharon!" The first season averaged over five million viewers. At the time, it was the most watched series MTV had ever had. It won an Emmy.
[RIFF: The Osbournes memories. Or use this:]
A whole generation met Ozzy as the mumbling dad on MTV. Their parents knew him as the guy who bit a bat. Same man.
And he never ran from the bat. Here's Ozzy to NPR in 2010, when he was promoting his memoir: "It's a stupid thing to do, but I did it."
And he leaned into it. On the 37th anniversary, in 2019, he sold a plush toy bat. Twelve inches, his logo on the chest, and a Velcro detachable head. Forty bucks, about fifty today. The first batch sold out. He announced it on Twitter: "Today marks the 37th Anniversary since I bit a head off a fucking bat!"

## THAT GUY
[CLIP: That Guy verdict]
Okay. Time to name him. That Guy.
The suspects.
Suspect one: Ozzy. The man who bit the thing. And, as we've established, not his first animal.
Suspect two: Mark Neal. The seventeen year old who brought a frozen bat in a plastic bag to a concert and threw it at a man known for biting the heads off birds. Plus his friend Carmen, who said "freeze it." Every bad idea needs a producer.
Suspect three: the dove. Not the bird's fault, obviously. But the dove story is what got around. That's what told every kid in America, this is the guy who bites things.
Suspect four: the meat. The catapult, the pig guts, the ads telling fans to bring their own. That's what taught the crowd to throw stuff back.
And the verdict.
I love Mark. Mark is awesome in the That Guy way. He owned it two days later, in the paper, by name. But Mark was seventeen, and Mark was doing exactly what the show trained everybody to do.
Ozzy bit it. But he thought it was rubber, because the show had taught him to expect rubber.
The moment somebody said, you know what this show needs? Guts. Let's throw guts at people, and let's ask them to bring their own. From that moment, a real dead animal on that stage was just a matter of time.
And that was Ozzy's show. His name on the ticket. His meat in the air. His dove that started the rumor. So That Guy is Ozzy. But the meat did it.

## THE LEGEND
Now, before we go. Ozzy died on July 22nd, 2025. He was seventy six.
Seventeen days before that, on July 5th, he played one last show, "Back to the Beginning," at Villa Park in Birmingham, his hometown. The original Black Sabbath, back together one more time. He couldn't stand for long anymore, so he sang sitting on a throne. Over forty thousand people in the stadium. The livestream peaked at about five point eight million people watching. The family says it raised around ten or eleven million dollars for charity, after they paid everybody's way in. Not the hundred and ninety million the internet said. Sharon called that number "just ridiculous."
So let's do the numbers.
Over a hundred million records sold, between Sabbath and solo.
"Blizzard of Ozz," the record he was promoting the day of the dove, is certified five times platinum in the US.
He's in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame twice. Once with Black Sabbath in 2006, once on his own in 2024.
His last solo record, "Patient Number 9," won the Grammy for Best Rock Album. He was in his seventies.
"Crazy Train" got played live over twelve hundred times, going by the fan logged setlists on setlist.fm. It never made the Billboard Hot 100 when it came out. The week after he died, it made it for the first time ever. Forty five years late.
On Spotify, he went from about twelve million monthly listeners to almost nineteen million in the days after he died. And in August 2026, "Crazy Train" passed one billion streams. His first solo song to do it. "Paranoid," with Sabbath, got there first.
[RIFF: what Ozzy means to you. Or use this:]
A billion streams. From a guy whose first job review was "good puke remover."
And Ozzy knew exactly how he'd be remembered. He said when he died, the headline would be, "Ozzy Osbourne the bat biter is dead. Bats can now live in peace." And he figured the bats would hear it and go, "Fuck me, thank God he's dead."
And sure, the bat made the obituaries. But so did Prince of Darkness. Godfather of heavy metal. Hall of Famer, twice. The guy who sold a hundred million records and filled a stadium in his hometown on a throne, weeks before he died.
Not bad for a cow killer from Aston.

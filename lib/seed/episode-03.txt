## COLD OPEN
January 20th, 1982. Des Moines, Iowa. A Wednesday night.
Ozzy Osbourne is on stage at the Veterans Memorial Auditorium, and something lands at his feet. Small. Dark. Thrown from the crowd.
People throw stuff at Ozzy all the time. Rubber snakes. Rubber spiders. Rubber everything. So Ozzy picks it up, figures it's a toy, and does what Ozzy does.
He bites the head off.
[Pause.]
It is not a toy.
And here's the thing nobody tells you about that night. It wasn't even his first time.

## WHO IS OZZY
Quick bit of context, for anybody who only knows Ozzy from the reality show, where he mostly yelled "Sharon" at a dog.
In the 1970s, Ozzy Osbourne was the singer in Black Sabbath. And Black Sabbath basically invented heavy metal. The sound, the darkness, the whole thing. Every band with a black T-shirt owes them money.
And by 1979, Black Sabbath fired him.
[Pause.]
Fired him. From the band that invented heavy metal. For partying too hard. In the seventies. Think about how much partying you have to do to get fired from Black Sabbath. That's like getting kicked out of a frat for being too loud. That's like getting kicked out of a strip club for being too horny.
[FILL: Sabbath kept going with a new singer, Ronnie James Dio, who was great. Ozzy, by his own account, holed up in a hotel and mostly just drank.]
And then a woman named Sharon Arden, whose dad managed Sabbath, decided Ozzy could be a solo star. She became his manager. Later, his wife. And she put a band together around him, with a young guitar player from California named Randy Rhoads.
Remember that name. We're coming back to him.

## THE DOVE
So it's 1981. Ozzy has a solo record, "Blizzard of Ozz." It's going to be huge, it's got "Crazy Train" on it, but nobody knows that yet.
And he has to go meet the record company. CBS. A boardroom full of executives. These are the guys who decide how hard they push the record, and whether this washed up guy from Black Sabbath is worth the money.
The plan, somebody's plan, is for Ozzy to walk in and release a couple of doves. Peace. Goodwill. "I'm a changed man." Real classy.
[Pause.]
Ozzy has been drinking.
He lets one dove go. It flies around the room. Lovely.
Then he takes the second dove, and bites its head off. In the meeting. In front of the people who sign his checks.
[RIFF: picture the faces at that table. Or use this:]
I need you to picture the guy whose job it was to arrange the doves. He had one task. "Get two doves." He got the doves. He did everything right. And he still had to explain to his boss what happened to one of them.
And they kept him. That was the job interview, and he got the fucking job.
Now, honesty check, because that's the rule. The dove story gets told a few ways. Some people say Los Angeles, some say New York. Ozzy himself has told it more than one way over the years. One version, he says the dove was already dead. But the core of it, Ozzy, executives, dove, no head: everybody agrees on that part.

## THE MEAT
So the record comes out, and it's a hit. Ozzy's back. And by the end of 1981, he's on the road, on the Diary of a Madman tour, with a live show built to shock.
And part of the show is meat.
He throws raw meat into the crowd. Actual meat. Organs. Guts. Whatever the butcher had that week.
[RIFF: the logistics of the meat. Or use this:]
Somebody on that tour had to buy the meat. Every day. In a new city. Walk into a butcher shop in Kansas and say, "Yeah, I'll take whatever you're going to throw away. A lot of it. No, I don't need it wrapped." That guy. What's his title on the tour credits? "Meat."
And here's what happens when you throw meat at a crowd. The crowd starts throwing stuff back.
Fans start bringing things to the shows. To throw on stage. Rubber snakes, toy bats, dead things. It becomes part of the deal. You go to an Ozzy show, you bring something gross, you throw it at Ozzy.
So by January 1982, a lot of stuff is landing at his feet every night. Most of it fake.

## THE KID
Now. Des Moines.
There's a seventeen year old kid in the crowd that night named Mark Neal.
And Mark has a bat. A real one. According to Mark, his younger brother found it and brought it home, planning to keep it. And the bat did not make it. It died.
[Pause.]
So the Neal household now has a dead bat. And an Ozzy concert coming up. And a seventeen year old who's thinking, "I know exactly what to do with this."
[RIFF: a seventeen year old in Iowa deciding the best use for a dead bat is a concert. Or use this:]
I want to talk about security in Des Moines in 1982. Because a kid walked into a concert with a dead bat in his coat. And nobody stopped him. Nobody said, "Sir, what's in the coat." I can't bring a bottle of water into a show in 2026, and this kid walked in with wildlife.
And sometime during the show, Mark throws the bat on stage.
[FILL: How do we know it was Mark? Because years later, he told the Des Moines Register himself. For decades this was just "some kid." Then one day the kid picked up the phone and said, "Yeah. That was me." I respect that. Most people would take that one to the grave.]
I keep thinking about the ride home. Mark's at the show with his friends. He's just thrown the bat. And then he watches Ozzy put it in his mouth.
[Pause.]
That's a quiet car ride home. Nobody's talking about the setlist.

## THE BITE
Ozzy sees it land. And remember, he's been getting rubber stuff thrown at him for weeks.
So he picks it up. Looks at it. Figures it's a toy.
And he bites the head off.
[Pause.]
It is not a toy.
Ozzy has described it a few times, and it's always the same feeling. His mouth was instantly full of something warm and awful, and he knew. Right away. That is not rubber.
[RIFF: Ozzy's face in that moment. Or use this:]
There's a moment, about half a second long, where a man's whole brain goes, "Huh. That's not what rubber tastes like." And then the rest of his life changes.
Now, there's always been a debate about whether the bat was alive. Mark Neal has said it was already dead when he threw it. So, the good news: Ozzy did not murder a bat on stage. He just bit the head off a dead one.
That's the good news. That's the best version of this story. That's the one you tell your mom.
And look. This is a man who bit the head off a dove in a business meeting a year earlier. So when a bat lands at his feet, the man has a certain level of experience. He's not new.

## THE HOSPITAL
After the show, they take Ozzy to Broadlawns Medical Center in Des Moines, and he gets rabies shots.
[Pause.]
And back then, rabies treatment wasn't one shot. It was a series. So for the rest of that tour, in every city, Ozzy had to find a doctor and get another shot.
[RIFF: the Prince of Darkness in a waiting room. Or use this:]
Picture the Prince of Darkness, in a waiting room, in Kansas City, filling out a clipboard. "Reason for visit." And he has to write it down. In pen.
[FILL: The story got everywhere. Newspapers, TV, parents' groups. For a while there were rumors about cities trying to ban his shows. Every parent in America now had a new reason to hate Ozzy. Which, from a record sales point of view, was great.]

## THE ALAMO
And here's the thing. Ozzy is not done. It's barely February.
About a month later, the tour is in San Antonio, Texas. And Ozzy gets arrested for urinating on a monument at the Alamo.
[Pause.]
The Alamo. In Texas. Where people take the Alamo very seriously. There's a whole saying about it. "Remember the Alamo." Ozzy did not remember the Alamo.
The story goes that he was wearing his wife Sharon's dress at the time, because she'd hidden his clothes to keep him from going out drinking. And he went out drinking anyway. In the dress.
That part I can't prove. But it's the most Ozzy detail that has ever existed, and I'm not taking it out.
[RIFF: of all the places in Texas. Or use this:]
Of all the walls in Texas. There are a lot of walls in Texas. It's a big state. He found the one wall.
He got banned from playing San Antonio for about ten years. Years later, he made it up to them with a donation, and they let him back in. Texas is more forgiving than you'd think.

## THE SERIOUS PART
Now I have to slow down.
Remember Randy Rhoads. The young guitar player.
On March 19th, 1982, two months after Des Moines, the tour is in Florida. And Randy gets on a small plane, for a joyride. And the plane crashes. Randy was killed. He was twenty five years old.
[Pause.]
Randy was the reason those records sounded the way they did. "Crazy Train." "Mr. Crowley." If you play guitar, you know. He was classically trained, he was obsessed with practicing, and he was just getting started.
[RIFF: what Randy means to you as a guitar player. Take your time. Keep it real, not a bit. If you'd rather keep it short:]
Every guitar player I know has spent at least one afternoon trying to figure out the "Crazy Train" solo. Most of us are still trying. That's what he left behind.
Ozzy has said losing Randy was one of the worst things that ever happened to him. So when we laugh about the bat, and we did, that same tour also had the worst day of his life in it.

## OUT
So that's the story. A dove in a boardroom, a bag of meat, a dead bat in Iowa, rabies shots in every city, and the Alamo. One very weird year for the Prince of Darkness.
And somehow, he made it. Decades more. A reality show. A whole second life. In July 2025 he sat on a throne in Birmingham, England, and played one last show with Black Sabbath, in his hometown. A few weeks later, he died. He was seventy six.
[Pause.]
For a guy who bit the head off a bat, that's a pretty good ending.

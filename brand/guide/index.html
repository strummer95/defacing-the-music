Defacing the Music

Host guide

For Duck and Pat. How an episode goes, what the cues mean, who does what, and the checklist before you hit record. Keep it open on your phone.

How an episode goes

  1. Cold open. About 30 seconds of quick teaser hits. No That Guy talk yet.
  2. Intro line. "I'm Duck, and due to [the title], this is Defacing the Music."
  3. Theme song. Hard stop.
  4. Out of the theme: "Episode [number]. [The Who ...? subtitle]." Then straight into the story.
  5. The story. Duck reads it. Pat hears it for the first time and reacts.
  6. That Guy. Suspects, verdict, the guitar squeal sting. "Because in every story there's a that guy. Don't be that guy."
  7. Sign-off. "I'm Duck. Due to unforeseen circumstances, I'll see you next time." Theme song. Done.

Script reading time is 20 to 30 minutes. With the two of you talking, the episode lands around 45 minutes to an hour.

Pat: your job

You're the listener in the room. Duck tells the story, you react to it. Like Gareth on The Dollop.

  • Don't read ahead or look it up. Your first reaction is the whole point. If you know the story, say what you thought happened, and let Duck tell you what really did.
  • Jump in whenever you want. You don't need a question from Duck. The scripts are packed with weird details on purpose so you'll want to.
  • Ask the dumb question. If something's confusing or you missed a name, ask. Every listener has the same question.
  • "Wait, what?" is a great line. So is "Who thought that was a good idea?" That's the whole show.
  • Call BS. If something sounds made up, say so. Duck has the source, and it makes the real stuff land harder.
  • Pick sides on That Guy. Disagree with Duck's verdict if you want. Arguing it is good audio.
  • Let the punchline land. If Duck is clearly building to something, let him get there, then go.

When the teleprompter shows [BEAT], Duck stops on purpose. That's your spot. You don't have to say anything, but it's yours.

Duck: reading tips

  • Reading is fine. Dave Anthony reads every Dollop. Read it like you're telling it at the bar, not like a newsreader.
  • Name the source when you read a quote. "Here's the Des Moines Register, two days later:" It's the rabbit-hole part of the show.
  • Stop at every [BEAT]. Look at Pat. If he goes, follow him. If he doesn't, keep reading.
  • If Pat goes off on a tangent, enjoy it, then "anyway" and pick the script back up where you left it.
  • Flubbed a line? Stop, take a breath, and say the whole sentence again from the start. That's an easy cut in editing. Don't patch it from the middle.
  • Riffs are optional. If the riff isn't there today, read the fallback under it. It's good enough as is.
  • Don't rush the That Guy verdict. Suspects, a beat, the name, the sting.

What the cues mean

Cues show up yellow on the teleprompter. You don't read them out loud.

[BEAT]
Stop and give Pat room. No question, just space.
[Pause.]
Duck's own dramatic pause, for timing. Not a Pat spot.
[TALK: ...]
A question Duck can throw to Pat. Rare on purpose. Skip it if Pat's already talking.
[RIFF: ...] Or use this:
Go off on it if you've got something. If not, read the line under it.
[FILL: ...]
An optional extra line in Duck's voice. Read it or skip it.
[CLIP: ...]
A moment meant for Shorts and TikTok. Nothing to do while recording; it's a marker for editing.
[THAT GUY STING]
Where the guitar squeal goes, right after the name. Added in editing.
[CUT TO: ...]
A picture or sound bite added in editing. Leave a second of space.
[THEME SONG]
Theme goes here in editing. "Hard stop" means it cuts off, then you start talking.
[NUMBER]
The episode number. The teleprompter fills it in once the script is made an episode in the admin.

Both of you

  • Don't talk over each other. A little overlap is natural, but long overlaps can't be cut apart in editing. Let the other guy finish the sentence.
  • Same distance from the mic every time: about a fist away, talking across it, not into the top. Lean back to laugh hard, lean in to talk.
  • Hands off the table and the mic stands. Taps and bumps go straight into the mics.
  • Great unplanned moment? Raise a hand on camera. It's easy to spot when scrubbing the video later for clips.
  • Don't explain the show while recording ("we should cut this"). Just stop, say the sentence again, keep going.
  • Water, no ice. Ice rattles.

Show rules (on air too)

  • No politics, race or hot-button topics. If a story touches one, say the fact and move on. No debate, no takes. Riffing included.
  • People who got hurt or died are never the joke. The joke is the guy who decided it was fine.
  • If it's not proven, it's "the story goes" or "the legend goes." Don't state rumors as fact, even in a riff.
  • No band talk. Duck's band never comes up on the show.
  • "Next time," never "next week."

Before you hit record

  1. Phones on Do Not Disturb. Both of you.
  2. Audio: AudioBox at 48 kHz (Windows sound settings and OBS must match, or you get the robot voice). Duck on track 2, Pat on track 3.
  3. Levels: talk normal and loud. Meters should peak in the yellow, never red.
  4. Cameras: Source Record filter on each camera (right-click the camera source, Filters), not on the scene.
  5. 10-second test. Record, stop, play it back with headphones. Check both voices and all three camera files.
  6. Teleprompter: open Read on the script in the admin. Check it shows the latest version.
  7. Hit record, then clap once, on camera. It lines up every file in editing.
  8. Mid-episode: glance at OBS View > Stats now and then. Dropped frames climbing means the computer is struggling.

After

  1. Stop recording and wait. Don't close OBS until every file finishes writing.
  2. Check the files are all there: the main recording plus one per camera.
  3. Back them up before anything else: second drive, or admin > Uploads.
  4. Write down anything great you remember (a Pat line, a riff) while it's fresh. Those are your first clips.
